# 27 / Burrow

2026-11-27

Into a burrow with a torch. It opens on the hole in a grassy bank under an overcast sky; inside, a small labyrinth of tunnels, roots through the roof, stones in the walls, and a hand-held beam that is never quite the right tool in a place this narrow.

Drag to look, hold to run (or `W`/`↑`, the wheel, a tap for one short dash). At a fork you go where you look. With a mouse the torch follows the pointer.

## How it is made

- **One field, one mesh.** The tunnels are capsules along a hand-drawn graph, stamped into a 3 cm grid together with the hillside and a few octaves of lumps, then meshed once with surface nets (`world.ts`). The same grid answers everything later: where the wall is for a root, where a crumb lands, how far the beam reaches. Two levels: the first goes in and round to the den; from its far west corner a long run goes down to a second, a metre lower and wetter, and back up. And the ground between is not solid. Besides the small hollows, four kinds of thing are dug out of the walls before the mesh is made, none of them a room: cracks (a hand wide, up to a metre tall, leaning, closing as they run off into the dark), chimneys up through the roof, pits in the corner of the floor, and low nooks where the floor runs on under an overhang for an arm's length. One carve does all four: a capsule whose distance is stretched or squashed along one axis.
- **The torch.** A cookie (hotspot, corona, faint reflector rings), inverse square, one 2048 shadow map from a lamp held beside the head, and a fake bounce light where the hotspot lands. The exposure is metered on the CPU like a camera would, nine rays, centre-weighted: point it at a wall close by and everything else falls into black; look back at the mouth and the day burns out.
- **The air.** Reused from day 20's light in the haze (`rays.ts` there, `fog.ts` here): a march from the eye to whatever each pixel sees, half resolution, a fixed dither, blurred on the way back up. There it asks the sun's shadow map; here the torch's, with its cookie and inverse square, so the beam is a cone in the air from beside the head and roots cut dark blades out of it. Steps crowd towards the eye, where the lamp is. The dust is the same air, only thicker: a fall of dirt, a paw, the start the animals give all put a puff of density into the march, which blooms, sinks and clears. A little of the day hangs in the air inside the mouth too. Outside there is none of this air at all, so the torch draws nothing on the sky, which is one flat pale grey. Dust is heavy: the air is thickest below the eye, over the floor.
- **Matter without textures.** One baked 64³ noise volume that stores its own gradient, so every octave of bump is a single fetch, and a second one of cells (nearest point, distance to the next, the cell's own numbers). Soil, stone, bark all read them at their own scales (`gfx.ts`). Octaves finer than a pixel are faded out instead of left to sparkle.
- **Ground that is not the same everywhere.** The first version showed its noise: one octave of lumps, repeating every hand's breadth. Now each octave is read through its own rotation, the middle ones twice at scales with no common measure, all warped by the octave below, so nothing lines up. And the ground lies in layers that buckle a little: dark crumbly topsoil for the first stretch in, clay below it (cracked where it has dried, bedded in fine level lines), thin seams of sand that have run out from between and left the wall undercut, stony seams and lenses that stand proud with every stone its own colour, and deep down ground that never dries, darker and greyed. The floor is trodden flat under its own dust. The same two lines of layer maths weather the mesh and colour it (`strataY` in `world.ts` and the soil shader).
- **What grows.** After day 16's moss (day 05's engine), copied over in `growth.ts`: a surface drawn again and again in shells pushed out along its normal, each strand a cell of noise, the same cell in every layer, thinning to its tip and leaning as it rises; and instanced strands brushed aside by whoever comes through. Three things grow, each where it would. Moss where there is light: the bank round the mouth and the first body length or two inside. Root felt, the mat of fine root hair, on roofs and upper walls, thickest round the roots, and it hangs. Mould where it is damp and dark, starting on what there is to feed on: roots, stones. The ground and the bark under the strands are stained by what is on them, the shells are only the strands (ten layers, the outermost drawn first so the rest hide behind it; layers whose strands would be finer than a pixel are thrown out whole in the vertex shader, and the pile is lit with one shadow tap and no gloss). From the roof hang some forty-five thousand strands of all three (beards of root hair and clumps of a dark, moss-like stuff, moss and dead grass by the mouth, threads of mould), and those and the grass at the mouth are parted by the head as it comes through, left and right of the way we go, roots staying put: the same vertex-shader brush that parts day 16's grass. All of it is for close up only, and thrown out in the vertex shader past that: a hanging strand is drawn within two metres of the eye and grows in between 2 and 1.4 m, the shells within three. Where water stands the air is wet: round every puddle, above its waterline, more mould on everything and a moss that needs no day.
- **A collar of grass.** The hole sits in one: tussocks all over the bank and thick in a ring round the mouth, the way in grown over with a short trodden sward, and only the lip of the hole itself bare. On that lip, all the way round but for the way in, thin herbs lean in over the hole and half hide it: a wiry bedstraw with whorls of needle leaves, a leafy creeper with a small blue flower, daisies and buttercups on bare stalks out of a rosette (`buildHerbs` in `outside.ts`). Every strip of every plant, its stem, each leaf, each petal, is one instance of one mesh; a leaf rides on its point of the stem's arc, so it sways and is brushed aside with it. None of the hillside is drawn once we are in: hidden from round the first bend, and before that whenever our back is to the mouth.
- **Wax caps.** Small, pale, slimy mushrooms (`fungi.ts`): troops of them on the wet ground round every puddle, and now and then out of a wall, likeliest where the mould is. One shape turned on a lathe, stem, gills and cap, instanced; each comes out of its surface and then turns up, as they do. White to grey, nothing brighter; the lamp sits on the cap as a wet highlight, and they throw their own small shadows. Not drawn past four metres.
- **A little water.** Six shallow bowls are pressed into the floor, well in and low down, and filled a little over half way: never more than a fifth of the ground within reach, so the floor's own lumps draw each shoreline and a sloping run does not flood. The bed shows through, wet and darker the deeper; a film of dust lies on the surface in drifting patches; the shore has a faint broken thread of light where the meniscus is turned right; what falls in sinks out of sight. The torch in it is only what a mirror gives. With the lamp at the eye that is two things: the lamp itself, as a soft spot, from the one angle where the surface sends it straight back (looking down into the water under our own nose), and at a flat angle whatever of the roof beyond the torch happens to light. Everything else that was tried to make it say water louder (light thrown on to the roof, threads of light crawling on the surface, glitter) read as caustics and confetti, and is gone.
- **Stones of more than one sort.** Eight ways of shaping one (blocks, many-faced, river cobbles, slabs, shards, nodules grown in lumps, a cobble broken in two, wedges: all a ball cut by planes with a softer or harder minimum) and six things to be made of: granite, sandstone bedded in coarse courses, slate in leaves, milky quartz with clearer veins, pitted ironstone, flint with a dark glassy heart under a chalky rind. Shape suggests the rock, neighbours tend to be of one seam, and where a big one came to rest there is rubble round it (`rocks.ts`, the rock shader in `gfx.ts`).
- **Hollows.** Dug into the field before it gets its lumps: pockets where something fell out, two or three run together into ragged ones, alcoves, and the narrow runs of smaller lodgers going off into the dark. Smaller holes still are pits in the shader.
- **The gait.** We go in the way the owner would, low and at a run. The stride is counted in ground covered, not in time: each forepaw takes the weight in turn, the shoulder on that side drops and the head tips with it (a roll of about 3°), the body swings over the planted paw, every landing is a dip and every push a surge in speed. Where a paw comes down, grit jumps. The torch trails the stride by a beat (`main.ts`).
- **What our passing does.** Fine roots are verlet strands with a memory of their shape; the head is a ball they cannot enter and they swing back after it. Loose stones on the floor get shoved along, set stones rock in their sockets, and the roof lets dirt go: over the path now and then, and wherever a root was tugged (`flora.ts`, `rocks.ts`, `dust.ts`).
- **The den.** Past the last node the field opens into a low chamber with a bed of straw and leaves (instanced bent strips). Whoever dug the place lies at the back with a young one: an icosphere shrunk onto melted ellipsoids, 44 000 short fur ribbons, and eyes with a tapetum term, light that arrives along the line of sight goes straight back, so with the torch beside the lens they are seen long before the animal is. They breathe and blink; she starts when we come in, the roof answers, the young one ducks (`den.ts`).
- **Debris.** The place is never quite still: dirt comes down ahead of us every metre or so, a little is always trickling somewhere within reach of the torch, and now and then a proper clod comes with the grit. What falls stays where it lands. On the floors lie bits of root and twig swept to the sides, loose stones, and near the mouth the leaves the wind has pushed in (`buildDebris` in `den.ts`).
- **The lens.** HDR frame, bloom, a little softness and falloff towards the corners, ACES, grain (`post.ts`).

## Learned

- A depth pre-pass for the ground more than halved the frame: the tunnels overlap on screen far more than it looks.
- The pre-pass must run the very same vertex expression as the lit pass (`invariant gl_Position`), or the two disagree by a hair and leave black pinholes.
- Metering only the ground made the animals burn out: the meter has to know what the hotspot lands on, and the pool of light gets a ceiling of its own.
- A torch next to the lens burns everything near it to white. Fine roots get a softened falloff, a cheat that reads as more real than the real thing.
