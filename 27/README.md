# 27 / Burrow

2026-11-27

Into a burrow with a torch. It opens on the hole in a grassy bank under an overcast sky; inside, a small labyrinth of tunnels, roots through the roof, stones in the walls, and a hand-held beam that is never quite the right tool in a place this narrow.

Drag to look, hold to run (or `W`/`↑`, the wheel, a tap for one short dash). At a fork you go where you look. With a mouse the torch follows the pointer.

## How it is made

- **One field, one mesh.** The tunnels are capsules along a hand-drawn graph, stamped into a 3 cm grid together with the hillside and a few octaves of lumps, then meshed once with surface nets (`world.ts`). The same grid answers everything later: where the wall is for a root, where a crumb lands, how far the beam reaches. Two levels: the first goes in and round to the den; from its far west corner a long run goes down to a second, a metre lower and wetter, and back up. And the ground between is not solid. Besides the small hollows, four kinds of thing are dug out of the walls before the mesh is made, none of them a room: cracks (a hand wide, up to a metre tall, leaning, closing as they run off into the dark), chimneys up through the roof, pits in the corner of the floor, and low nooks where the floor runs on under an overhang for an arm's length. One carve does all four: a capsule whose distance is stretched or squashed along one axis.
- **The torch.** A cookie (hotspot, corona, faint reflector rings), inverse square, one 2048 shadow map from a lamp held beside the head, and a fake bounce light where the hotspot lands. The exposure is metered on the CPU like a camera would, nine rays, centre-weighted: point it at a wall close by and everything else falls into black; look back at the mouth and the day burns out. Out in front of the hole, and only there, the lens has a depth of field (a 30-tap gather in `post.ts`, each place giving only what its own circle of confusion would spread that far): the grass against the lens and the bank behind go soft, the hole is sharp. It is gone a body length in. It is carried, not mounted: at a run it swings with the stride and drops to light the ground just ahead. And its cells are tired: every so often, sooner when it is shaken about, it sags to a fraction, stutters and comes back, and the meter does not make it up.
- **The air.** Reused from day 20's light in the haze (`rays.ts` there, `fog.ts` here): a march from the eye to whatever each pixel sees, half resolution, a fixed dither, blurred on the way back up. There it asks the sun's shadow map; here the torch's, with its cookie and inverse square, so the beam is a cone in the air from beside the head and roots cut dark blades out of it. Steps crowd towards the eye, where the lamp is. The dust is the same air, only thicker: a fall of dirt, a paw, the start the animals give all put a puff of density into the march, which blooms, sinks and clears. A little of the day hangs in the air inside the mouth too. Outside there is none of this air at all, so the torch draws nothing on the sky, which is one flat pale grey. The hillside itself is lost in that same grey before it reaches the edge of the grid, so it has no edge. Dust is heavy: the air is thickest below the eye, over the floor.
- **Matter without textures.** One baked 64³ noise volume that stores its own gradient, so every octave of bump is a single fetch, and a second one of cells (nearest point, distance to the next, the cell's own numbers). Soil, stone, bark all read them at their own scales (`gfx.ts`). Octaves finer than a pixel are faded out instead of left to sparkle.
- **Ground that is not the same everywhere.** The first version showed its noise: one octave of lumps, repeating every hand's breadth. Now each octave is read through its own rotation, the middle ones twice at scales with no common measure, all warped by the octave below, so nothing lines up. And the ground lies in layers that buckle a little: dark crumbly topsoil for the first stretch in, clay below it (cracked where it has dried, bedded in fine level lines), thin seams of sand that have run out from between and left the wall undercut, stony seams and lenses that stand proud with every stone its own colour, and deep down ground that never dries, darker and greyed. The floor is trodden flat under its own dust. The same two lines of layer maths weather the mesh and colour it (`strataY` in `world.ts` and the soil shader).
- **What grows.** After day 16's moss (day 05's engine), copied over in `growth.ts`: a surface drawn again and again in shells pushed out along its normal, each strand a cell of noise, the same cell in every layer, thinning to its tip and leaning as it rises; and instanced strands brushed aside by whoever comes through. Three things grow, each where it would. Moss where there is light: the bank round the mouth and the first body length or two inside. Root felt, the mat of fine root hair, on roofs and upper walls, thickest round the roots, and it hangs. Mould where it is damp and dark, starting on what there is to feed on: roots, stones. The ground and the bark under the strands are stained by what is on them, the shells are only the strands (ten layers, the outermost drawn first so the rest hide behind it; layers whose strands would be finer than a pixel are thrown out whole in the vertex shader, and the pile is lit with one shadow tap and no gloss). From the roof hang some forty-five thousand strands of all three (beards of root hair and clumps of a dark, moss-like stuff, moss and dead grass by the mouth, threads of mould), and those and the grass at the mouth are parted by the head as it comes through, left and right of the way we go, roots staying put: the same vertex-shader brush that parts day 16's grass. All of it is for close up only, and thrown out in the vertex shader past that: a hanging strand is drawn within two metres of the eye and grows in between 2 and 1.4 m, the shells within three. Where water stands the air is wet: round every puddle, above its waterline, more mould on everything and a moss that needs no day.
- **A collar of grass.** The hole sits in one: tussocks all over the bank and thick in a ring round the mouth, the way in grown over with a short trodden sward, and only the lip of the hole itself bare. On that lip, all the way round but for the way in, thin herbs lean in over the hole and half hide it: a wiry bedstraw with whorls of needle leaves, a leafy creeper with a small blue flower, daisies and buttercups on bare stalks out of a rosette (`buildHerbs` in `outside.ts`). Every strip of every plant, its stem, each leaf, each petal, is one instance of one mesh; a leaf rides on its point of the stem's arc, so it sways and is brushed aside with it. None of the hillside is drawn once we are in: hidden from round the first bend, and before that whenever our back is to the mouth.
- **The game.** It sits on a nest, and in the nest is one egg (`game.ts`). It cannot be walked up to, so it has to be drawn off: stand in its run until it starts for us, go back up, and round the loop of tunnels behind it (F, J, P) to come at the nest from the other side. It follows the way we went, not the shortest way, which is what makes that work. Lift the egg (walk onto it) and it knows: it stops crawling and makes for the first fork in from the mouth, the one place every way out passes, and bars it; it only leaves that for us when we are within three metres, and then it comes faster than it crawled (faster still when we cannot see it, though never as fast as we run). So the way out takes leading it round a loop too. Caught, we are dead: it all begins again at the mouth, and the egg is back in the nest. Out under the sky with the egg, everything fades and there is the egg, turning in a light of its own, with a thought. All of it was run through in fast-forward on the rails (the beast's own update, fed a scripted player) to see that it can be done: the nest reached at 11 s, out at 31.
- **Wax caps.** Small, pale, slimy mushrooms (`fungi.ts`): troops of them on the wet ground round every puddle, and now and then out of a wall, likeliest where the mould is. One shape turned on a lathe, stem, gills and cap, instanced; each comes out of its surface and then turns up, as they do. White to grey, nothing brighter; the lamp sits on the cap as a wet highlight, and they throw their own small shadows. Not drawn past four metres.
- **The other lodger.** Something lives on the lower level (`beast.ts`; the model is a Blender script, `model/beast.py` -> `public/beast.glb`, rigged, five clips): a mole's body, a bald scaly wedge of a head, clouded eyes, a jaw of needles, claws. At load every deep stretch of floor is scouted for the longest clear look down a tunnel at it (five rays a hand apart, so a chink through a wall does not count), and it waits at the far end of the longest: the bottom of the long straight run. From the top of that run, four metres off, it is two points of light and nothing else: its eyes send the lamp back without the inverse square, and its hide is kept black until we are within three metres. It will not be walked up to: from 3.5 m the legs slow and at about 2.5 they stop. Stand there a moment and it starts towards us, crawling (there is no clip of it walking: the legs are swung on top of the idle, diagonal pairs together), coming out of the dark as it comes. It is in no hurry (a quarter of a metre a second while we can see it, twice that when we cannot). We can turn and run, and it comes after us: it goes about the burrow on the same rails we do, by the shortest way through the tunnels to wherever we are, so there is nowhere down here to lose it. Only the day it keeps out of: three metres of tunnel from the mouth it stops, where the dark begins, and waits. Wherever we stop it comes right up, stands a body length off in the lamp for two or three seconds, then gathers itself and comes through the air mouth first. We go over backwards, the view tips up to the roof, the torch is thrown about in the hand, cuts in and out, and is out. And that is the end of us: black. Out of the black its eyes, twice, right over us (they burn by themselves then); the torch catches for a tenth of a second, three times, and it is still there, mouth open; black again. After seven seconds it all begins again in front of the hole. The hide is a skinned mesh in the scene's own light (scales are cells with a dark seam, in the hide's rest space so they stay put as it moves); the wiry fur is day 05's shells again, here the same skinned mesh bound six more times to the one skeleton. `?beast` starts at the top of its run, looking at it.
- **A little water.** Six shallow bowls are pressed into the floor, well in and low down, and filled a little over half way: never more than a fifth of the ground within reach, so the floor's own lumps draw each shoreline and a sloping run does not flood. The bed shows through, wet and darker the deeper; a film of dust lies on the surface in drifting patches; the shore has a faint broken thread of light where the meniscus is turned right; what falls in sinks out of sight. The torch in it is only what a mirror gives. With the lamp at the eye that is two things: the lamp itself, as a soft spot, from the one angle where the surface sends it straight back (looking down into the water under our own nose), and at a flat angle whatever of the roof beyond the torch happens to light. Everything else that was tried to make it say water louder (light thrown on to the roof, threads of light crawling on the surface, glitter) read as caustics and confetti, and is gone.
- **Stones of more than one sort.** Eight ways of shaping one (blocks, many-faced, river cobbles, slabs, shards, nodules grown in lumps, a cobble broken in two, wedges: all a ball cut by planes with a softer or harder minimum) and six things to be made of: granite, sandstone bedded in coarse courses, slate in leaves, milky quartz with clearer veins, pitted ironstone, flint with a dark glassy heart under a chalky rind. Shape suggests the rock, neighbours tend to be of one seam, and where a big one came to rest there is rubble round it (`rocks.ts`, the rock shader in `gfx.ts`).
- **Hollows.** Dug into the field before it gets its lumps: pockets where something fell out, two or three run together into ragged ones, alcoves, and the narrow runs of smaller lodgers going off into the dark. Smaller holes still are pits in the shader.
- **The gait.** We go in the way the owner would, low and at a run. The stride is counted in ground covered, not in time: each forepaw takes the weight in turn, the shoulder on that side drops and the head tips with it (a roll of about 3°), the body swings over the planted paw, every landing is a dip and every push a surge in speed. Where a paw comes down, grit jumps. The torch trails the stride by a beat (`main.ts`).
- **What our passing does.** Fine roots are verlet strands with a memory of their shape; the head is a ball they cannot enter and they swing back after it. Loose stones on the floor get shoved along, set stones rock in their sockets, and the roof lets dirt go: over the path now and then, and wherever a root was tugged (`flora.ts`, `rocks.ts`, `dust.ts`).
- **The den.** Past the last node of the first level the field opens into a low chamber with a bed of straw and leaves (instanced bent strips), heaped and pressed into a hollow. Whoever made it is not in it: there were two here once, an old one and a young one, and then something else moved in below.
- **Debris.** The place is never quite still: dirt comes down ahead of us every metre or so, a little is always trickling somewhere within reach of the torch, and now and then a proper clod comes with the grit. What falls stays where it lands. On the floors lie bits of root and twig swept to the sides, loose stones, and near the mouth the leaves the wind has pushed in (`buildDebris` in `den.ts`).
- **The lens.** HDR frame, bloom, a little softness and falloff towards the corners, ACES, grain (`post.ts`).
- **Sound.** A small WebAudio engine (`sound.ts`), started by the first press or key, asleep while the tab is hidden, left out with `?mute`. Texture is sampled, what answers us is synthesised. Sampled: fourteen short takes from ElevenLabs' sound effects model (the table below), never played the same way twice: a slice of a take, at its own pitch, placed left or right by where the thing is to the head. The meadow on the bank (and gone a few metres in), the room tone inside, a paw on packed earth, dirt coming down wherever the scene lets some go, a stone knocked, drips (every pool on its own slow clock, each drop heard from its pool by the metres of tunnel to it: all of it within one, a faint plink with the tunnel's slap in it at eight, to one side only when near), a splash when a paw lands in one, our own breath (discreet: none at rest, a hint at a run), and the other lodger: its breath, a low growl now and then while it watches, a snarl that does not stop while it is after us, a groan every so often, and its call: thin and high, rare (every ten or twenty seconds while it is down here with us, once when it sets off after us, once before it leaps, and one long one through the whole burrow when its egg is taken), which is there to say where it is: all the way to one side when it is to that side, and quieter, duller and with more of the tunnel's slap in it the further off it is, out to twenty metres of tunnel. Synthesised: the weight of each forepaw and the hind paw after it, grit or grass under it, crackles in the earth and the ground settling, the wind, the heart (a low triangle struck twice a beat, unheard until there is something to fear, then quicker), a drone of two low voices a hair apart with a slow pulse under the beast's breath, its paws (a heavy pad, grit, the click of the fore claws, each at its own point of the walk's cycle, lighter when it scuttles, heard a little further off than its breath), its lunge and its jaws, and the torch's tired electronics: a thin whine while the cells sag and a tick at every stutter. The beast is heard by how far it is through the tunnels, not through the earth: a trace at eight metres, all there is at one, to one side only once it is near, duller behind us, nothing of it from out on the bank. Dead, there is the heart, racing and then slowing, and nothing else. The place is one short convolver: a third of a second of noise that goes dull as it dies, behind a few reflections some milliseconds out. One `update(dt)` at the end of the frame reads what the scene already knows (pace, inside or out, the beast, the torch, the pools) and moves the mix thirty times a second; nothing is allocated per frame. Every sample has a synthetic stand-in, so a missing file costs texture and nothing else. The mix was set by reading meters, not by ear: the levels are in `MIX` at the top of the file, and `burrowSound.mix` in the console turns them while it runs.

### The samples

All in `public/audio`, made with ElevenLabs sound effects (`eleven_text_to_sound_v3`, prompt influence 0.5, one take each), then cut, filtered, levelled and encoded once from the raw take with ffmpeg: 128 kbps mono, but for the meadow (192 kbps stereo) and the room (128 kbps stereo). The takes come out of ElevenLabs as 128 kbps MP3 and some very quiet (the meadow needed 23 dB of gain), so what coding there is to hear in them now is the original's.

## Learned

- A depth pre-pass for the ground more than halved the frame: the tunnels overlap on screen far more than it looks.
- The pre-pass must run the very same vertex expression as the lit pass (`invariant gl_Position`), or the two disagree by a hair and leave black pinholes.
- Metering only the ground made the animals burn out: the meter has to know what the hotspot lands on, and the pool of light gets a ceiling of its own.
- The ten layers of moss and felt were most of a frame, and not for the reason it looked like. Cutting them into pieces by place and only handing the near ones to the GPU took the triangles from 7.9 to 4.8 million a frame, and the frame hardly moved: the cost was the noise test that decides whether a strand is there, run for every layer over every pixel. Working the bar out first and fetching each noise only if the ones before it could still clear it keeps exactly the same pixels and halved what the layers cost (a frame at the fork went from 42 to 24 ms at 2160x1350).
- Measuring that took a script of its own (the built page in headless Chrome on the real GPU, the frame loop stopped, each group hidden in turn, medians of three): the browser pane's timings are noise.
- A torch next to the lens burns everything near it to white. Fine roots get a softened falloff, a cheat that reads as more real than the real thing.

Hold E to turn the head and look behind (the legs keep going the way they were, the torch comes round with the head). `?debug` puts up sliders for the film grain, `?clean` hides the furniture, `?beast` starts within sight of the thing on the lower level, `?egg` starts with the egg already in hand (try `?egg&at=G,0.6`), `?win` shows the last screen, `?touch` forces the phone's start card, `?auto` crawls in by itself, `?at=N,1.4` starts 1.4 m short of a node (`N` is the den), `?flat` shows the blockout shading, `?nofog` the frame without the lit air, `?mute` leaves the sound out.
