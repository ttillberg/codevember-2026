# 20 / Cocoon

2026-11-20

A preservation pod hanging by its cables in a hall. It is heavy: a push nudges it a few degrees and it swings slowly, the cables follow, the door that never quite shut knocks on its hinge, and blood runs off it all the time.

- **Model** (`model/build.py` → `model/cocoon.blend` → `public/cocoon.glb`): built from a turnaround sheet by a script (the sheet lives in `model/ref/`, kept out of the repo; `compare.py` needs it there), in Blender or without its window, two minutes with the bake:

  ```sh
  cd 20/model && /Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b cocoon.blend --python build.py
  pnpm -C 20 glb   # then pack it: textures to webp, meshes meshopt-compressed and quantized (3.9 MB to 1.6)
  COCOON_STAGE=look Blender -b cocoon.blend --python build.py --python compare.py -- out.png   # no bake, held up against the sheet
  ```
  - `sheet.py` is what the sheet says, read off it by hand on a numbered grid: where each view sits on the sheet and its scale (the shell is 2.6 m tall without the cables, which sets it), the outline of the body in the front, back and both side views, and every plate traced as an outline in the view it is seen best in.
  - The form: the front and side outlines stood up as prisms and intersected (the front one made even first, the same half-width both sides at every height: the sheet's front is lopsided and the pod leaned like a parallelogram from behind, where the sheet has it even), then cut by a few slanted planes through points measured on the sheet: the ridge down the front where the seam is, the strip beside it, the 03 shield looking down 11 degrees so the line between them runs on the slant as the sheet draws it (and not flat: it folds 7 degrees along a line from its top down past the left of the 03 to its foot, leaning forward as the sheet draws it, and again across its stepped upper part, turned up towards the cap), the hatch, the right flank, the back corners, a ring round the top and one round the bottom. Everything comes in by a plate's thickness, so the plates' outsides are the outline.
  - The plates: every traced outline cuts the surface along the line of sight of its view, and each piece of surface belongs to the view it faces most. Its plate is the first traced outline it falls in; `open` is no plate. Where a side view sees machinery and the front sees plates, on a face turned well to the front, the front wins: the sheet does not agree with itself there. The 03 shield's own facet behind its stepped edge is turned more to the side, so there the side view wins and it is machinery, as the side view has it. Each plate is pulled in from its edges for the seams and given 6 to 8 cm of thickness, folding where the form folds.
  - Everything else is placed by pointing at a pixel of a view: a ray from that pixel finds the surface. The open back (a spine with three ribs and lighter plates on it: the block with two lamps, the tall maker's plate with warning marks, a seam and a boss either side, the foot with a V; two pairs of heavy hoses down it, sleeved in rings, in octagonal collars and a clamp that holds each pair, bending in at the foot and rising off the back to the ceiling as cables; thinner ones with couplers down the pockets either side, round fittings by the spine's head, brackets, a bar across, the manifold, specks of warm light; the sheet's white strips down the back corners were tried and taken off, they stood off it like wings, so the back is machinery out to the flanks' plates), the open bottom (a frame round a sunk block, ribs, a ring of boxes, hoses, two lamps), the machinery behind the shields (base plates, ribbed panels, boxes, round fittings, thin hoses and lamps laid in by a seeded random, each piece flat on the surface with its whole footprint on open surface and the taller ones clear of the hoses; boxes and hoses leaning with the shield's stepped edge, a third off upright, the heavy hose bending forward at the foot; clamps, lamps), every hose fitted the same way (each end it has goes into a steel coupling on a foot, a collar every 28 cm bolted down by a bracket; the flank hoses, a pair side by side near the flank's back edge, start in couplings up top, run straight and steep down it and bend sharply forward into a box at the foot; hoses are drawn in a view's pixels, sampled every 25 px onto the surface and eased so the surface's folds put no kinks in them, which lies in the flank's own plane as the leaning boxes do), a window high in the hatch that is not on the sheet (dirty frosted glass in a thick steel frame, a dark board of diodes behind it of which only the lit ones' glow comes through, blurred: three.js transmission with roughness), the latch and blocks in the seam, the deck on top with nine brass-collared ports, the runs of blood down the seam.
  - Small things, twice what there was: each plate is cut into panels along its own lines, not the world's (a fine seam, 1 cm, where the sheet's plates have them): the way its edges run most sets the way, one seam runs down the middle along it, and it is cut square across near both ends, or once through the middle of a short plate, every plate edge is chamfered 1.6 cm, and each panel that is not on the top or the bottom gets one thing by a seeded random: a raised hatch with connectors on it (a round one with a flange, a nut and pins in a dark face, two of those, or a flat multi-pin block in a frame; no handles), a dark slot in a steel frame, a vent of slats, a small tag with two diodes, or nothing; only where its whole footprint lands flat on that panel, clear of the seams and the window. The door has a hinge: knuckles along its line, the door's and the pod's in turn, a leaf from each. The machinery behind the shields is twice as dense: more base plates, ribbed panels, boxes (half with a smaller one on top), fittings, thin hoses and lamps.
  - `model/decals/`: the day's title card traced by potrace with sharp corners, cut into `day-20.svg`, `codevember.svg`, `year.svg`, `strip.svg`, all of it in `codevember-20.svg`. Tried on the shields in place of the 03s and taken off again; `Marks.svg()` still lays an svg flat at a point if one is wanted.
  - Markings are off for now (no 03s, texts, crosses, stripes, discs, UEC). `markings()` still lays them all if they are wanted back: (03, TYPE-A, STASIS PRESERVATION, the stasis mark and bars, RETECH HUMAN FURTHER, CONTAIN PRESERVE ITERATE, UEC, crosses, the split disc) the same way: each one shot along its view's line of sight and laid flat on the face it lands on, 1.5 mm above the plate. The left shield's group sits a little back from where the side view has it, halfway to where the front view has it; the right shield's further back, so it does not show from the front, which the front view says it does not.
  - Bolts: round every panel, 2.6 cm in from its edge and one every 13 cm or so along it, one small hex head (8 mm), nearly flush (not in the glb as geometry: where each goes, on an empty, and the web side draws them all as one instanced mesh, as it does the collars along the hoses), only where it lands flat on its own panel and clear of the other edges at the corners and of the window; four on each hatch and vent.
  - Wear is baked: noise in object space for dirt, runs, specks and scratches, grime heavier towards the foot, dust on what faces up, chips along the edges found with the Bevel node, an AO bake for dirt in the corners, the seams and round the bolts. Two 2k textures, colour and roughness + metal, for the plates only.
  - Inside: something wrapped, strapped to a cradle on the back wall. A red light in there breathes, slowly, in for two and a half seconds and out for four: it lights what the open door shows, and comes out round the door, the raw metal in the seams glowing with it (only within a few centimetres of the door, in the door's own space; the open flanks are machinery and stay dark), and through the frosted window. It has no shadows, so only what is in there takes its light (the wrapped thing and the shell's inside; every other material is compiled without the point lights), or it would shine out of every seam.
  - `compare.py` renders the model at the sheet's own scale and angle, draws its outline over the sheet's picture with the traced one, and prints how much they overlap: front 94 %, sides 93 and 92, back 92. The sheet's views do not quite agree with each other, so that is about as high as it goes.

  The web side knows nothing of the shape, only names:
  - `pod`: the body, everything that swings is its child
  - `door`: child of `pod`, origin on the hinge, hinge along its local Z
  - `plug.NNN`: empties on the pod, local Z the way the cable leaves, custom props `radius` and `slack`
  - `socket.NNN`: empties in the ceiling, one per plug
  - `hang`: empty, the point it all swings from
  - `lift.NNN`: empties on the pod, the eyes of the lugs it hangs by
  - `drip.NNN`: empties where it bleeds

  Export the `cocoon` collection as glb with modifiers applied and custom properties on.
- **Materials** (`materials.ts`): the glb only names its materials; the web side makes each what it is. The plates are painted metal (the baked wear) under a thin satin clearcoat; the machinery gunmetal, the core under the plates a darker raw metal, clamps, collars, couplings, lugs, connectors and the wires it hangs by brushed black metal, the bolt heads dull zinc, brass the brass; panels and boxes a dark plastic, the housings a light one with a little gloss, black parts matte plastic; the cloth inside has a sheen. The hoses, on the pod and up to the ceiling, are black rubber with a dusty sheen and ribs: their uv runs along them one rib every 1.3 radii (set in `build.py` and `cables.ts`), and a bump of that, fading out where the ribs get finer than the pixels. Nothing is even: a grit of noise in object space (world space for the hall's instances) changes how rough each surface is and its tone a little.
- **Hall** (`hall.ts`): made up, not in the reference. Lattice steel, every bar one instance of a box: towers either side going up into the haze, box girders across and along at three heights, a catwalk on each side, the roof 75 m up, bands of glass in its middle and a solid deck 24 m wide all round its edge, in the haze. The structure goes on in every direction, a grid of towers seven across and eight deep out into the haze, girders between them at every level round the pod and at every other one further out, staggered; on the sun's side a far wall 60 m off with small openings in a few rows. At the end the eye looks at, 72 m off, a wall with three huge vents in it, 14 m across, between the towers, 53 m up between the girders: a plate cut round, a drum through the wall, three struts holding the motor, five broad pitched blades turning slowly (each its own speed, the middle one the other way), daylight behind. Scaffolding round a dozen of the towers, here and there and part of the way up (tubes on a 2 x 1.3 m grid, a lift every 2 m, braces, boards every third lift), and five hoists: a narrow mast up the side of a tower, a cage riding up and down it, waiting at the ends. The girder the pod hangs from runs across the hall; a trolley runs along it with the block and sheave and a grating round it where the hoses come down. It moves the pod: five metres along one way, a wait, the hoist winds it two metres up, a wait, down, then right across to five metres the other way and the same, and back, eased and slow, two minutes and a bit round, with the hoses' tops on the trolley. The pod swings back as the trolley sets off and on as it stops (its pull on the pendulum, no more than a slow trolley can give), and the eye goes along with it.
- **Sun** (`rays.ts`): the light that comes down through the structure. The roof is bands of glass between strips of deck, bars across the glass, and the far wall has its small openings; the sun stands high on the right and a little behind, and only those let it in. Its shadow map (fitted tight round the hall as the sun sees it, a rectangle up to 8k on its long side, about 1.7 cm a texel; the debug panel's sun direction moves the sun but not the fit) lights the hall and the cables; the pod has a sun of its own, the same light again with a map only 4.4 m across that follows it (2 mm a texel), and each material takes one sun and not the other (a gain on the first two directional lights in three's light loop, swapped for the pod's materials, `materials.ts`). The wide map does two things: the steel and the pod are lit in bands and bars where it gets through, and the light in the haze is marched against it: from the eye out to whatever each pixel sees, clipped to the hall, 24 steps, each asking the shadow map whether the sun reaches there, thicker higher up, brighter looking into the light, the sun's light used up on its way down from the roof so the shafts die out before the floor, with a glare right under the glass, sixteen times the rest and gone within a few metres, and the air itself patchy: smoke and dust as two layers of slow noise drifting through it. 24 steps at half resolution with a fixed dither, blurred on the way back up, added before the tone mapping. And daylight through the vents in the end wall, in the same march: no shadow map for it, the fans are worked out. At each step, back along their light (in a little down and across) to the wall, and how open a fan is where it gets there: shut outside the drum, on the hub, behind a blade (broad at the tip, a little narrower for its pitch) or a strut, the blades turned as far as the meshes are. So the beams turn with the fans, cut in five, used up within about 13 m. The depth is the scene's own (`post.ts`).
- **Light**: all from above and the sides. One skylight right over the pod (a narrow spot slanting down from the front left through the girder's gap, the only light with shadows), a cold window in the left wall, a warmer one low on the right, a little fill. The fog is lit from above: three.js's fog chunks are swapped for one that thickens to a lighter grey the higher it is (the scene's fog is a `THREE.Fog` whose `near` is the density and `far` how much brighter it is up high, so both can be set live), so the steel stands dark against the haze up there and sinks into the dark (#0a0c11, a little cold, a grey with some steel blue in it that the haze takes up the higher it is) below; a dome behind it all has the same grey at each height.
- **AO**: baked. The plates' paint has it (in its colour and its ORM texture); everything else on the pod has it as a colour on each vertex, an AO bake in Blender (`bake_vertex_ao`), lifted a little so a corner is dim, not black. The screen-space pass (three's GTAO) is off: it cost a third of a frame on a big screen and the hall did not need it; the debug panel's AO (O) brings it back to compare.
- **Shafts**: the light of the skylight over the pod and the side windows in the fog, faked: open cones from each light, drawn additive, thin at the rim, fading along their length and near the eye, with streaks of noise drifting down them. Dust drifts down through the one over the pod, faint, a few hundred motes. The small windows in the sun's wall are in the haze like everything else, so they do not stand out of it as specks.
- **Camera**: the first view is the hall picture's: from below, looking up at the pod's left side and front, the pod hanging plumb under its hook (it once hung askew, head to the left, as the hall picture has it; a pod on four slings from one hook does not). On a wide screen it hangs left of centre. Drag to orbit, right drag or shift drag to pan, the wheel to come closer or go back.
- **Eye**: drag to turn round the pod, wheel or pinch (two fingers) to go nearer or farther, right or shift drag to move sideways. The debug panel's Copy view (C) puts the eye, the pod, the crane and the screen on the clipboard as JSON, with a `look(...)` to get back to it (on the phone, over the local net, the page is not secure: then the old way, a selected text area).
- **Leaf shutters** (`hall.ts`): behind every fan, outside the wall, five blades, each a plate whose inner edge is one side of a five-sided opening; they slide in from five sides, turning a little as they come, until they meet in the middle, two seconds and a bit, eased. The light through the fans is worked out the same way in the haze's march: inside that five-sided opening or not.
- **Roof shutter** (`hall.ts`): as on a shop front, but over the whole roof, 126 m of slats between the deck and the glass, rolling up onto a drum in a housing at this end. The page starts with it rolled up, but only half: open, it still covers the half of the roof on this side, so only a little light comes in; the button shuts it, slow to start and to stop, about twenty seconds, the hall going dark band by band of glass, and opens it again, the light coming back from the sun's side over to here. It is not in the wide sun's map (drawn once): its shadow is worked out where the light is, on every lit material (a function in three's light chunks: up the sun's light from the point to the shutter's height, under it if it gets there between its ends) and in the haze's march, and the pod's own sun has it drawn in its map as well. The skylight over the pod and its shaft come on as the edge passes over them; the haze's brightness up high, the fill, the light through the fans and windows and the windows' faked shafts come up with how much of the roof is open.
- **Controls** (bottom left, `main.ts`): the hall's machines, one round button each with an abstract symbol in yellow. The first is the roof: a bar that rolls onto a dot as the shutter rolls up, and light falling under it; press it to shut or open. Then the fans in the end wall, three rings in a row, the light in each ring closing to a point as their leaf shutters shut. The other pods and the hoists (`pods.ts`) are made to be driven from here, next.
- **The twin** (`pods.ts`): the twin of this one (its outside only) hangs close, from a trolley under the girder just behind this one, to the right as the page opens (near enough to show through the haze), its number painted on it in a script no one here reads (ten glyphs of a few strokes, as this one has its own on its flank). Four other pods hung there (a ball, a capsule, two balls, a box) and were taken out.
- **Pod**: a pendulum under `hang` (two leans and a twist), not a rigid body. Held still (the default), no air moves it and a push dies out: let go and it swings back once, heavily, and settles. Drag it and a stiff spring holds it to the hand. It hangs by steel: four lugs bolted to its shoulders round the deck (`lift.NNN`), a sling from each up to one link on the line to the hang point, one wire from the link up to the block. The hoses only feed it: they hang slack from a ring round the hang point.
- **Cables** (`cables.ts`): verlet ropes, both ends pinned, the plug end by two points so the cable leaves the way the plug points. Bending is a soft link to the point two and four further on, which spreads a bend out instead of kinking. They are pushed out of the pod taken as an ellipsoid. The pod moves the cables, never the reverse.
- **Door**: a plate on a hinge under gravity, less the acceleration of the hinge, so it flaps when the pod is thrown about; a tired strut pulls it to, a dry hinge holds it where the pull is small, a stop keeps it from shutting. It rests a few degrees open whichever way the pod hangs. Drag it open: the drag follows the way the grabbed point would move on screen.
- **Blood** (`blood.ts`): it never stops. A bead hangs at each drip point and a thin run comes off it, 34 bits a second at full flow: young bits stretched long enough to overlap, a thread, that pinch into drops at a random age as they fall and speed up, the way a stream breaks up. The flow swells and thins on two slow sines, a swing throws more out, and new bits leave with the point's own speed, so the stream bends behind the swing.
- **Debug panel** (bottom right, in dev or with `?settings` / `?debug`; open unless you folded it away last time: a chevron in its head folds it to a gear): views as on the turnaround sheet, front `1`, side `3` (again for the other side), back `9`, the usual perspective `0`, each easing round to the pod however it hangs; in the sheet's views it hangs straight and still, as the sheet draws it. Iso or perspective `5`. The reference `R`: the screen split beside or below, with the sheet's matching view, or the hall picture in perspective (from `model/ref/`, so only on the dev server). Hold still `H` (on from the start: no swing, no crane round, until it is let go), rays steps 24 / 32 / 40, hall steel with its grit or plain (to set them against each other), AO `O` (the screen-space one, off: about a third of a frame on a big screen; the light in the haze reads the scene's depth itself, so it stays), and a readout of the door, the swing and where the camera is (yaw, pitch, distance, what it looks at), to set the first view by.
- `?debug` also opens a panel for the look (`tweaks.ts`, lil-gui): tone mapping and exposure, the fill, the haze's colour, density and brightness up high, the sun's strength, colour and direction, its light in the haze, how fast that is used up going down and how smoky the air is, the other lights, the faked shafts, the AO. What is set is kept in the browser until reset; "copy as json" puts it on the clipboard to write back into the code.
- **Frame time** (an M2 Pro, 2048×1536, measured by syncing on each target): 42 ms at first, 16 now, nothing to see given up for it.
  - The hall's sun map is drawn once, with only what stands still in it (the steel); the pod and its cables cast into the pod's own sun, which the light in the haze also asks, but only for what stands within a few metres of the pod along the light: that map has the steel too, sharp to the millimetre where the hall's blurs thin bars away, and the air in its column came out darker than round it, a dark box round the pod.
  - The steel is in blocks of the hall (16 × 24 × 15 m), each an instanced mesh with its own bounds, so the eye, the pod's sun and the skylight draw only the blocks they see; the frosted window's pass draws none of them (only the pod's inside is behind it), and at a third of the size.
  - Each material leaves the other sun out of three's light loop altogether, its shadow lookup and all.
  - At most 60 frames a second (a 120 Hz screen would draw it all twice as often), and a budget of 4.5 million pixels: a 16-inch retina screen full has 7.5 million, and every pass pays for each. There, full screen: 37 ms before, 25 after (the AO about 9, the light in the haze 5, the scene about 11).
  - The grit on every surface, the smoke in the rays and the faked shafts sample a tiling 3D noise texture (`noise.ts`) instead of hashing sines.
  - The frame (`post.ts`) draws the scene once with 4x MSAA and its depth into a texture; the AO reads that depth (normals from it) instead of drawing the scene again, at half the drawing buffer; the passes after the scene write plain targets, not multisampled ones the size of the screen. The eye's far plane is 100 m: past 90 m nothing shows through the haze.
  - The debug readout has the frame rate, the frame time and the size of the drawing buffer.
- `?clean` hides the title and the panel. `?silk` is the first take on the word: one thread of silk spun around a glow (`silk.ts`).
