# 24 / Nest

2026-11-24

Seen from above, close in: eight snakes of eight species, each about a metre long (a green tree python, an albino Burmese, a ball python and a jungle carpet python; a corn snake, a California kingsnake, a Honduran milk snake and an eastern garter snake), piled on a patch of ground half a metre across so the screen is nothing but skin, crawling slowly through the knot without ever undoing it. The knot is baked by physics offline, the heads are modelled in Blender, the scales are a shader. It loops: every 90 seconds the knot is exactly where it started. Tap a snake and its scales play; hold one down and it gets away, the ones lying next to it too, and slows back to a crawl when let go.

## How

- **The drawing** (`paths.ts`, `bake.ts`): eight closed loops on the ground, Lissajous figures (1:2 up to 3:4) of random size, place and tilt, knocked a little off true, with an S-wave laid along them as a snake's body lies, each drawn to about 1.1 m, packed onto 0.54 by 0.36 m (turned a quarter on a portrait screen). Strict over-under-over along every loop can't be had with eight of them, half the crossings fight and the fights build towers; so each loop gets a slow wave of height along its length instead, and at a crossing the one higher on its wave goes over. Never a fight, and every snake still weaves over and under the others.
- **The bake** (`bake.ts`, `bake/run.ts`, run with node): each loop a closed ring of spheres a centimetre apart, all eight let fall into one another under position based dynamics with all the time and iterations it needs: gravity eased in, held to the drawing in x and z firmly while the pile forms and then barely, so it slumps out to two or three deep the way a heap does, crossings resolved up and down in their order, no bend tighter than twice the body's radius (tighter and the skin folds into itself), every sphere against every other one, colliding 8% wider than drawn so what a pile presses in never shows. Sixty knots are tried quickly and scored (screen covered with skin, nothing in anything, not too deep), the best four settled again hard, the best kept as `src/tracks.json`: every loop's resting place in three dimensions, and where nothing lies over it.
- **Gliding** (`physics.ts`): each snake fills four fifths of its track and glides along it, a train on its rails, as a snake's body goes exactly where its head went: nothing to solve at run time, nothing that can shake or sink in. Once round its loop every 90 s, in pushes and pauses that add up to exactly one lap; the head comes up to look round only where the track is clear above. All of it periodic, so the whole knot loops.
- **Holding one**: press on a snake (on a snake a drag doesn't turn the camera) and hold: after a moment it gets away along its track, faster the longer it is held, up to about 20 cm a second, and calms down over a few seconds once let go; the snakes lying next to it start too, less. The dash is distance on top of its crawl, so the crawl's pushes and pauses don't stutter it, and it is still on its own track: a scare never undoes the knot.
- **Body** (`body.ts`): a tube swept through the chain each frame, a snake's cross-section (flat belly, spine ridge on the python, round at the neck to fit the head). Per vertex: where it is on the skin (scale coordinates that never swim), how stretched it is on the outside of a bend, and occlusion from the ground and the other bodies. Contact shadows are the spheres drawn from above into a canvas under the ground.
- **Species** (`species.ts`): the four boids get the thick tracks, the four colubrids the thin ones, the biggest species on the thickest. Each has its scale rows, gloss, eyes (a slit for the boids, round for the colubrids, red for the albino), tongue and pattern: the python's green with its broken white spine line, the corn snake's saddles, the ball python's caramel blotches rimmed in gold, the carpet python's black and yellow, the Burmese's butter-yellow net, the kingsnake's white bands widening down the flanks, the milk snake's rings, the garter's three stripes and checks.
- **Skin** (`skin.ts`): `MeshPhysicalMaterial` plus a shader. Dorsal scales are staggered rows of rounded rhombi, overlapping like shingles with the one nearer the head on top, a thin shadow under every free edge, each scale at its own slight tilt so they catch the light one by one; belly plates one per vertebra. On a stretched bend they part and skin shows. Colour is decided per scale from where its centre is, so the corn snake's saddles and black borders follow scale edges. When a scale is smaller than a pixel its tilt turns into roughness instead of shimmer, judged by the mean of its two sides so a foreshortened edge keeps its scales. Clearcoat for the keratin, a touch of iridescence on the python.
- **Camera and look** (`post.ts`): ambient occlusion from the depth buffer, at half size: 16 points in the hemisphere over each pixel, its normal from the nearer of its neighbours so an edge doesn't smear, a blocker counted less the further in front it is so a body high in the pile throws no halo; smoothed over neighbours at the same depth and laid on the colour before the blur, so the creases of the pile and the joins between bodies go dark. On top of the per-vertex occlusion the bodies already carry. Then a macro lens's shallow depth of field, from the depth buffer: each pixel gathers round itself on a golden-angle spiral out to its circle of confusion, a sample counting only where its own circle reaches, and one behind a sharp pixel only as far as that pixel's own circle, so nothing blurs over what is in focus. The focus is pulled, a little late as by hand, onto whatever is in the middle of the screen until you click: then onto the bit of body clicked, and it stays on it as the snake moves, or onto the ground where you clicked. Then the grade: a soft S curve, cool shadows and warm highlights, a touch more colour, a lifted black, a vignette, fine grain. The camera is held in the hand: a slow sway and breathing on top of where the orbit controls put it, never quite repeating.
- **Heads** (`model/heads.py` → `public/python.glb`, `public/corn.glb`, `public/heads/*.jpg`): two parametric heads in Blender, run headless, a python's and a colubrid's; the other six species each get their own colour map baked on the same scales of their family's head. Eye sockets, brows, the lower jaw tucked under the lip in the mesh. Scales are a weighted Voronoi on the surface: hand-placed shields (rostral, labials, and for the corn snake the colubrid crown: internasals, prefrontals, frontal, supraoculars, parietals) and Poisson granules elsewhere, relaxed twice, baked into colour, normal and roughness maps with the heat pits, nostrils and mouth line. Eyes and a forked tongue are in the file too; the eyes get their iris and pupil in a shader (a vertical slit for the boids, round for the colubrids) and the tongue flicks.

`?clean` hides the plate. `?loader` holds the page on what shows while it loads: the knot as a wireframe drawing in the title's ink, drawn at once while the real shaders are built off the thread (one skin program for all eight snakes, a uniform picking the pattern), the heads come in, the textures go up and each part is drawn once unseen, then the picture fades in over the drawing in 1.6 s with nothing left to build. The index clip and share picture (`about/screengrab.mp4`, `.png`) are played by `about/demo.md`: `pnpm grab 24 --clip`. `?debug` opens a panel of the look's knobs (the occlusion, also shown alone; the lens; the grade). The scales as pixels. Tap a snake and its own pixel animation goes off in its scales where you tapped, twelve frames a second: a star for the Burmese and the kingsnake (a dot, four crossed rays to a *, the middle going out, the tips running on alone), a ring for the green tree python and the milk snake, a diamond for the ball python and the garter, a plus for the carpet python, and for the corn snake its scales scattered: round the tap each scale wears another's colour a few scales off, shuffled anew every frame and knocked askew, the shuffle spreading and dying down. Drawn against the pattern, no ground of its own: each scale of an animation or a letter in whichever of two of the snake's own colours stands out more from its own, light on dark and dark on light. When it is done, two of them write a line out of the scale tapped, in a 5 by 7 font running toward the head at four scales a second, once: the kingsnake CODEVEMBER 2026 - @THEO_T, the corn snake to Wim and a heart, red but where it lands on red.

## Learned

- Physics alone doesn't keep a knot, a knot alone doesn't make physics. The crossing rule is the bridge: only at a crossing, only when the two strands touch, resolve vertically and in the knot's order.
- Live physics in a pile always shakes a little and always presses a little in, and no amount of smoothing fixes both. Run the physics once, offline, as hard as you like, and keep only where it came to rest: a snake follows its own head anyway, so a body gliding on a baked track is not a cheat, it is how they move.
- Overlap is what makes scales read as scales. Tiled rhombi looked like a bathroom wall; anterior-on-top with a shadow under the free edge looked like a snake.
- From across the room the scales are gone, but their roughness isn't: dropping it made the python a balloon.
- Smooth what you draw, never what you keep: a relaxing pass fed back into the eased spine piled up frame on frame, shrank every bend and flattened every hump over a crossing, and the bodies sank 3 cm into each other.
- The springing wasn't the springs. It was two rules fighting: crossings a few centimetres apart asking for over-under-over, and a sphere tagged with one crossing shoved sideways by the other. Keep whoever is on top on top, and the fights stop.
