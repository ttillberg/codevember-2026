# 07 / Hollow

2026-11-07

Paint on a hollow, clear balloon. WebGPU through three's node materials (TSL) in React Three Fiber. The brush sits at the centre of the screen and you grab the balloon and spin it under the brush, any way, like a trackball. It keeps turning once let go, slowing only a little, a flick along speeds it up and a flick against slows it; grabbing never stops it. Hold the button to paint, or switch on auto (the button, or space) and the brush never lifts: a still hand gives a ring and a moving one a spiral. Six tools: a pencil, a round brush, spray, a bristle brush, a glaze and a scraper; three finishes: matte, gloss and metal. Thick wet paint runs down the balloon, evens out sideways and dries where it stops.

## How

- **Paint layer**: two half-float MRT targets in the balloon's UV space, ping-pong. `color` holds premultiplied rgb and coverage, `props` holds thickness, wetness, roughness and metalness (the last two times coverage, so they move with the colour). Dabs are queued through the frame and splatted in one instanced draw of quads, One / OneMinusSrcAlpha into both targets, so a dab sets its finish where it lands and leaves the rest. The scraper is a second draw with Zero / OneMinusSrcAlpha: everything under the blade is scaled down. MRT outputs default to no blending in three; `setBlendMode` with `MaterialBlending` on each output is what makes them take the material's.
- **Run pass**: a full-screen quad reads the current target and writes the next, as many steps a frame as it takes to keep the pace at 120 a second. Down is the room's down: each frame the world gravity is taken into the balloon's frame with the inverse of its quaternion, and the pass projects it per texel onto the tangents of three's sphere mapping (u round the axis, v from the bottom pole), which gives a direction in texture space and a strength that is what is left after the part straight into the surface. A texel above a run threshold sends its excess one texel along that direction, split between its x and y neighbours by the direction's components, scaled by its wetness; colour, roughness and metalness travel with the thickness as fractions. The threshold is noise in lanes along the flow, fine across and coarse along, so sheets break into drips whichever way they run. Arriving paint is wet, resting paint dries at a constant rate, wet paint is blended a little across the flow so runs round off. Thickness is capped.
- **Drips**: the long legs are particles, not the run pass. The brush piles its paint up in a coarse map as it lays it (one pass of the default brush comes to about 1.3 per cell, drying out of it slowly), and a cell over a threshold (lower the more liquid, jittered per spot) lets a drop go: the drop gathers the pile around it over a brush's width, give or take, which sets its mass and keeps the next drop from forming right beside it. Each frame the drop slides along gravity on the sphere (the same tangent projection as the run pass, in JS), lays a thin dry trail behind it once it is out of the stroke it came from, loses mass with the distance, and where it stops, spent or on a part of the surface gravity has no grip on, it leaves a bead. Most are small and stop soon, a few go far. The run pass still makes the soft tongues along a stroke's lower edge; a cellular model on its own drained into sheets rather than collecting into drops, and the mass audit (`paint.total`) showed why each fix fell short: a thickness cap that clipped a growing drop, sideways spread that dissolved a narrow stream, a rate under 1 that left a tenth behind in every texel, and a stream rule that bled strokes into pale lines.
- **Pencil**: just colour. A smooth round dab with no thickness and no wetness: no bump, no run, no drip, no bloom, and none of the watercolour either, since the wash look is keyed on the glaze's glass-smooth finish, which no other brush lays.
- **Spray**: a dense core where the jet hits and speckles of overspray around it, fewer the further out. The speckles are keyed on the texel, not the dab, so the dozen dabs that overlap along a stroke hit the same ones and they build up into grain; keyed on the dab they averaged into a smooth fog. Now and then a tiny dot lands off to the side. Spray lays flat, no relief, and a moving stroke never drips, whatever the liquid: only the can held still piles anything up, a dab every 50 ms at the spot and the cloud widening to twice the brush over a couple of seconds, and its drops are thin dry legs with a small bead.
- **Glaze**: a watercolour wash. The dab is a soft disc whose rim is pushed in and out by noise in target space, so the edge of a stroke is ragged the way a wet edge dries, and the same across every dab. It lays no thickness and no wetness, so it never bumps, runs or drips, and its finish is glass-smooth; low flow, so a pass is translucent and builds up. In the balloon shader a wash (no thickness) gets its pigment ring: where the coverage sits above the mean of its surroundings a couple of dozen texels out, just inside the rim, it is denser, wide and soft like a coffee ring. No grain: the water does the texture.
- **Wet on wet**: a wash is laid wet and floats out in the run pass while it is wet. Each step a thin wet texel first evens out a little with its neighbours, so the dabs melt into one wash, then grows towards the most any neighbour a few texels off has, channel by channel (the most, not the mean: a front growing towards a mean of three blanks and one colour is a quarter as strong each hop and dies in two). The growth runs through a water field of two octaves of noise, squared, so the bloom pushes out in broad fingers with dry gaps between; it is capped by how far the water has carried it, and where the water is running out the pigment may sit a little above its neighbours, the darker outline a wash dries with. Where there is colour already it bleeds in, which is how two washes mix. The wetness goes along, a little less each hop, so the front dies out on its own a brush or two away. Neighbour wetness is clamped: laid wetness blends on top of what is there and reads over 1 straight after a dab, which once sent the bloom's reach to 8 and whited out the balloon.
- **Watery**: a slider for the glaze: wider and softer dabs at less flow, and more bleed, so a wash spreads thin, stays translucent and mixes with what is under it; at zero a firm blob that hardly floats.
- **Texture**: the paint target is 2048 × 1024 by default; a setting in the menu (LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH) picks 1024, 2048 or 4096 and reloads, since the targets and every shader are built on the size. Every distance set in texels (the lanes, the pigment ring, the bump offset) scales with it, so the paint looks the same, only finer. The run pass keeps its steps a second, so at 4096 the runs move at half the pace: scaling the steps as well spiralled, a slow frame asking for more steps, down to 11 fps.
- **Liquid**: one slider maps to the run threshold, the lane contrast and width, the run rate, the drying, the spread, and the drip chance and leg length.
- **Balloon**: a sphere with a front mesh and a back mesh (BackSide, drawn first) so the paint on the far side shows through. Clear rubber has no diffuse at all: black colour, under 1% opacity plus a thin fresnel border (f^7), a faint pale emissive rim as a stand-in for the room. Paint coverage is the larger of the dab coverage and thickness × 2.5, so runs read as paint. Wet paint is glossier. A tiny procedural equirect studio (gradient plus two soft boxes in a data texture) is the environment, so gloss and metal have something to reflect.
- **Relief**: the normal comes from the paint's height, thickness plus a fine noise grain scaled by thickness, differenced one texel each way in the target (not screen-space derivatives, which are resolution dependent and noisy) and fed through `normalMap` as a tangent-space normal. Dabs lay the paint in ridges along the stroke (noise on the load), so round strokes get orange peel, bristle strokes get furrows, and runs get a lip.
- **Breathing**: an attract mode. Left still, the balloon breathes: a slow swell (it stays a perfect sphere, always), a slower bob and a lazy sway, a couple of percent. It eases in over a few seconds and drops out the moment it's held or spun, so in use it keeps its shape and its place. Cmd and drag turns it without painting.
- **Wheel**: the balloon's orientation is a quaternion and its spin an angular velocity vector in world space, carried on with next to no drag. While grabbed, each pointer step turns it about the axis across the step (a window width is half a turn), and the hand's angular speed over the last few events is added to the velocity on release. A soft dark disc on the floor stands in for a shadow. Every frame a ray through the screen centre is cast at the balloon and the hit's uv is fed to the stroke, dabs laid along the way at 6% of the brush size with a low alpha each, so a dozen overlap at any point and the stroke builds up smoothly instead of popping in dab by dab; the stroke's angle comes from the uv step. Round and bristle dabs share the stroke's seed so furrows run on, spray reseeds per dab, and the impasto noise is taken in target space so overlapping dabs agree. The ring on screen is the brush's radius projected from the hit point. The runs follow the room's down, so turning the balloon turns the drips.
- **Poles**: a texel in u is next to nothing on the sphere near a pole, so every u offset the passes take (the bloom's neighbours, the melt, the spread across a run, the pigment ring, the bump) is stretched by 1 / sin(theta), clamped at 8, and the water field is sampled in roughly even coordinates, so washes don't pinch into rays there.
- **UV mapping**: dab width is divided by sin(πv) so dabs stay round near the poles, dabs within reach of the u seam are drawn again a turn over, and strokes take the short way round the seam.

## Learned

- `renderer.clear()` on an MRT target leaves alpha 1 on the second attachment. Clear with your own quad.
- An orthographic pass into a WebGPU render target comes out mirrored in v against the sphere's uv. Mirror the quads, and make them double sided, since the mirror turns their winding.
- `readRenderTargetPixelsAsync` returns raw half-float bits for HalfFloatType targets. Decode them yourself.
- The paint looked washed out for an hour until a probe showed the numbers were right: 60% coverage of red over a light page *is* pink. Measure before theorising.
- Bright lights on a white, nearly transparent diffuse read as milk, because 5% of a 3× overexposed white is still a lot. Clear materials want a black diffuse.
- R3F 9 takes an async `gl` factory, so `WebGPURenderer` + `await init()` just works. Three's TSL typings are thin around `vec4(vec3, float)` and attributes; a few `as any` keep `tsc` quiet.
