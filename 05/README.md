# 05 / Moss

2026-11-05

Sow moss on a white faceted boulder, or on one big rounded letter or digit, and watch it settle in. Every object starts bare, the rock on load and anything you pick: all the moss on it is yours. The button top left shows a live miniature of the current object, moss and all, with a moss meter ring around it: the share of reachable surface covered by mature moss (age over 0.55, so the thin rim of a stroke does not count), area-weighted, not counting anything that faces down or infertile spots. Cover 80 % of the rock and the alphabet unlocks with a burst of moss confetti (the palette's moss balls), the ring fades away, and the miniature opens a picker; until then there is no picker, and no numbers anywhere. Each glyph in the picker is its own miniature: a low-detail bare rounded letter in the rock's white material, no moss, rendered one per frame in the background, green text standing in until it lands. The unlock is remembered in localStorage. The picker has the rock and every glyph in the three.js Helvetiker typeface, regular weight: rounding and moss thicken every stroke, and in bold the small counters (A, e, 4) close up. The bevel also grows the outline less than it is deep, for the same reason. The default view is from the front with the eye almost on the ground; the rock floats a little above its shadow. The camera fits the object's height into 70 % of the viewport; the rock also fits 95 % of the width, so it works on a portrait phone. Drag on the rock to sow, shift-drag (or right-drag) to scrape it off, drag outside the rock to orbit (the cursor is a cross where a drag paints and a hand where it rotates); on a phone one finger paints, two fingers turn it and pinch zooms in a little. A small "two fingers to rotate" hint sits just under the object and fades out after the first two-finger turn. The default brush is coral, the green reindeer moss. The mix brush is auto: it mixes species by the surface normal (tops get cushion, sides feather, undersides dark star moss, lichen in its own patchy field), by stroke speed (slow strokes press in thick cushion, fast ones leave thin feather and lichen), by a slow time drift, and by a random bias rolled per stroke. The swatch row at the bottom, in order feather, star, cushion, lichen, coral, mix, maps to keys 1 to 6. Hovering a swatch shows the real plant it is modelled on: *Hylocomium splendens* (glittering wood-moss), *Polytrichum commune* (common haircap), *Leucobryum glaucum* (pincushion moss), *Cladonia rangiferina* (grey reindeer lichen) and *Cladonia arbuscula* (green reindeer lichen). The last two are lichens, not mosses. These are the closest real models, not exact matches. The eraser at the end of the swatch row (or X, or shift-drag) scrapes, since there is no shift key on a phone. Cmd-drag (ctrl on Windows) sideways sets the brush size, a ring shows it; it starts medium. The moss itself is scaled to 175 %; a hidden slider (add the `show` class to `.scale`) goes 50 to 200 % and covers frond and straw lengths, fuzz height and frequency, bulge. H hides the rock so you can look at the moss alone. A small debug panel top right, on the dev server or with `?debug` in the URL, toggles the back light, the sky ambient and the translucency and has a fill button that covers the whole object with the current brush at once, for comparing looks.

## How

- **The M**: an extruded, bevelled letter shape, one midpoint pass then three Loop passes so every edge rounds off into a soft pillow, about 2.4 units tall. The bare surface is lit with smooth vertex normals, the geometric normal is only used under moss where the mounds are. Anything indexed can become a paintable surface: `finishSurface` computes normals, adjacency and the per-vertex fields. The rock builder is still in `rock.ts` if you want a boulder back.
- **Undercoat**: shell texturing, the fur trick, but as one model for all four species. The base layer takes a velvet shade (dark facing the eye, lime at grazing angles, a sheen term, noise bumps) and the 16 shells above are a noise density field that thins upward: an irregular soft fuzz, no strand grid, no stipple. Every per-species number (height, age curve, density, sheen, palette) sits in a small table and is blended by the vertex weights, so a mixed zone is a real in-between rather than a switch.
- **Fronds**: 140k instanced ribbons (40k on touch) on top, for the curly leaves shells cannot do. The geometry is a flat strip; the vertex shader bends it along a cubic bezier with a random lean and a hooked tip, adds a side branch, and serrates the edge. Instances are scattered by triangle area times fertility so none are wasted on bare rock. Each instance stores its nearest rock vertex and reads the moss age from a float `DataTexture` that mirrors the vertex attribute, so painting and growth drive both layers.
- **Species**: five weights per vertex, not an index, so they mix. The fifth, coral, is green reindeer moss: the lichen's branching build, shorter, thicker and spread into round clumps, in deep green. On the shells the weights are one interleaved buffer read as a vec4 plus a float; the frond shader gets feather, star and lichen from the age texture, cushion from the puff texture, and coral as the remainder. Cushion moss has no fronds at all, it is the undercoat alone with taller shells and a stronger sheen, and its own shape: a round dome with a steep shoulder and a flat crown, and only the gentlest undulation, smooth with no emboss, rounder and softer than the others, in an even clear yellow-green with shallow depth and fine uniform hair, no dark patches. On top of that a static 3D cell field (Worley) per vertex splits it into gentle round clumps with soft, slightly darker valleys between them, like a bed of pincushions. The clump profile is a smoothstep, rounded at both the valley and the crown: a sharp V at the crevice gets interpolated linearly across the triangles and shows the mesh as straight ridges. Its fine grain is smooth noise in rotated frames instead of the voxel hash the others use, which read as a grid on the domes; the straws ride the same clumps; the fourth weight is the remainder of the other three so the RGBA age texture still fits. The brush adds weight for the current species with the brush falloff, a slow blur between neighbours softens any edge for a couple of seconds after a touch, then holds still so nothing keeps changing behind your back. Each frond instance draws its species from the weights against a smooth noise field rather than a random number, so a 50/50 zone becomes patches of each, not salt and pepper. Shells blend colour and height continuously. The frond template holds a stem, a branch and six short strips: feather moss uses stem + branch, star moss turns the six into a rosette of stiff pointed leaves and thins itself to 55 %, lichen uses all eight as a curving stem with twigs forking off along it, every instance, longer and wider than the mosses, over a taller paler fuzz. The age texture is RGBA: age, then the three weights.
- **Second pass**: brushing over moss that is already there rearranges it. The pillow field is a live per-vertex copy, and each stroke shifts it under the brush with a noise offset rolled per stroke, so the mounds move around a little. On top of that a small, stiff, critically damped spring gives the surface a firm little push, no wobble. Fronds read the live pillow field from the same float texture as the puff.
- **Moss normals**: the rock keeps its crisp facets, but the moss grows along a separate normal field, the vertex normals smoothed over ten rings of neighbours. Shells, domes and fronds all extrude along it, so across a sharp edge the direction turns gradually and the shell layers never fan apart into streaks.
- **Mounds**: no extra mesh needed, the rock already has 42k vertices. Both the shell vertex shader and the frond vertex shader push the surface out along the normal by age² times a shared pillow field, so mature moss has real volume. The ground and fuzz are lit with the screen-space geometric normal, which gives real shadow between the pillows, and that contrast is what makes the green read as vibrant. The raycast picker stays on the undisplaced mesh; the offset is small enough not to matter.
- **Swatches**: each brush button is a pea-sized stone completely covered in that moss. At startup a unit sphere with the same shell and frond shaders, moss everywhere, fronds and fuzz scaled up about 25x so they are in proportion to a pea, is rendered one species at a time into a render target with a transparent background, read back into a canvas and set as the button's image. The mix swatch uses noise patches of all four. No frames, no pill, just the balls and their labels. On phones the row becomes an infinite swipe carousel: the centred ball is the brush, one neighbour visible on each side, flick to spin. Scrape is a little two-tone rubber eraser rendered in 3D the same way as the balls, next to the row, top right on phones. The object toggle and the hint are hidden for now.
- **Straws**: about 1 % of frond instances are a single tall, very thin grass straw instead of their species' geometry, on any species including cushion moss. A low-frequency field marks straw-rich areas, roughly a tenth of the surface, where it is closer to 9 %, and upward-facing surfaces get both more straws and much longer ones. Letters only grow straws in those straw-rich areas, so they do not get grass along every top edge. Height and bend are random per straw, most nearly straight. Cheap volume for the silhouette.
- **Shadow**: a real shadow map. When the word is built the scene is rendered once from the light into a 2048 depth texture with an orthographic camera that fits the word, and the shell and frond shaders sample it with 16 Poisson taps spread over three texels, so the penumbra is wide and soft. Letters shadow each other and the moss under overhangs. Lighting is directional rather than wrapped, with a low ambient, so sides facing away from the light go properly dark.
- **Grade**: the only colour correction is a small grade on the moss, in linear light before the sRGB encode: 1.3x exposure, 1.35x saturation, a little contrast around mid grey. The white surface is left alone so it never clips.
- **Patches**: brightness follows height and normal: lime at the top and on upward faces, deeper green down the sides, and a slight darkening towards the bottom. The mounds carry shape, not colour, so they never read as stripes. A static low-frequency noise field per vertex (and per frond instance, same function) adds darker patches, but only low down, so it never stripes the top. Yellow only shows up as a random sprinkle of single fronds and single fuzz strands, never as a patch.
- **Painting**: raycast against the rock, write into a per-vertex `moss` attribute (0–1 = age). No UVs, no render targets.
- **Key light follows the camera**: the sun turns with the view around the vertical axis, same elevation, always from the front right, so every side of the object is lit the same way and the translucency and shadows do not change character as you orbit. The shadow map is re-rendered every two degrees of turning, not every frame. Swatches and glyph thumbnails keep the fixed base light.
- **Back light**: a second light hangs behind the object as seen from the camera, a little above it, and follows the view. It only reaches the silhouette, through a tight Fresnel term: the fuzz tips and the fronds along the outline glow lime against the background. Fronds read the outline from the surface normal under them, not from their own ribbon normal, or every leaf turned edge-on would light up.
- **Sky ambient**: where the key light does not reach, moss takes a cool blue sky fill from above and a warm, greenish bounce from below instead of flat grey; sunlit faces keep their colour.
- **Translucency**: thin moss lets the key light through, strongest when looking towards the light and partly on the side facing away from it, weighted towards the strand tips. It shows mostly as a yellow-green glow on the shadow side; the camera sits low and the light high, so looking straight into it is rare. Since the light follows the camera, it looks the same from every side.
- **Hollows**: each object bakes an occlusion value per vertex once at build time. The vertices go into a voxel grid and each one marches 24 rays over its hemisphere, counting hits within 0.35 units, a few moss thicknesses. In holes and inner corners the shells and fronds then grow shorter and lower and sit darker, so a letter's counters stay open instead of filling with moss.
- **Growth**: painted vertices start at 75 % of their cap, so there is no sparse seedling stage, and age up the rest over a few seconds. The cap is a per-vertex value set by the brush falloff, so a stroke is tall in the middle and domes down at the edges instead of ending in a wall. Age which drives height and colour. It used to spread to neighbours through an adjacency list, but that made the patches feel out of control, so now it stays where you put it.

## Learned

- Shells beat raymarched SDFs for anything fibrous. Sphere tracing hates high-frequency detail; discard does not care.
- `#include <colorspace_fragment>` in raw `ShaderMaterial`s, otherwise your clear colour and your shader colours disagree.
- The picker is a second invisible `Mesh` sharing the geometry. Raycasting an `InstancedMesh` with 32 instances is pointless work.
- 160k fronds at 26 verts each ran at 20 fps. Tiny triangles are the cost, not vertices. 90k at 18 verts, placed only where moss can grow, runs at 100+.
- Scale sells it more than detail. Fronds a third of the size and the rock suddenly looks like a boulder instead of a pebble in a shrub.
- I spent twenty minutes hunting a "GPU lookup bug" that was a seed sphere poking through the far side of the rock. Check the dumb thing first.
