# 06 / Orrery

2026-11-06

An orrery in React Three Fiber. The eight planets are glass cat's eye marbles on their real orbits, the sun is an incandescent bulb, everything stands on a dark test grid. Time runs at a day, a week, a month or a year per second; the date starts at today. Keys: space pauses, 1 to 4 set the speed, P cycles the scale, O cycles the camera (perspective, orthographic front, isometric; the flat views are for checking the rings' tilt), L shows names.

## How

- **Orbits**: JPL's approximate Keplerian elements (valid 1800 to 2050) with their rates per century, Kepler's equation solved by Newton, rotated into the ecliptic and then into three's y-up frame. Positions check out against the sky: early November 2026 has Venus just past inferior conjunction, Neptune entering Aries, Uranus in Gemini.
- **Scale**: four modes, P cycles them. `sqrt` (default) sizes the bodies by the square root of their real radius, the sun doubled, the planets tripled (`PLANET_SIZE`), and spaces the orbits like the arms of a real orrery: each orbit keeps its true shape (r scaled by base / a) and its base is chosen so its perihelion clears the previous orbit's aphelion, marbles and rails included. `sizes` uses real relative sizes: the sun swallows everything out to Mars. `true` uses one linear scale for both, which leaves only labels on screen, Jupiter is 0.0004 units across. `orrery` is the hand-picked first pass.
- **Marbles**: cat's eye marbles. A transmissive glass sphere around three or four broad vanes in the planet's colours, each a blade that fills most of the disc, cupped across, bent along and gently twisted, with wandering white streaks and lighter edges as vertex colours, plus a few trapped air bubbles. Shape is rolled from a seed per planet name. The vanes add a little of their own colour as emission, since opaque glass still lets light through and the far side would otherwise go black. Glass thickness is kept low: at 1.6 the sphere lenses the vanes up until they fill it. Real axial tilt, Venus and Uranus spin backwards, the on-screen spin is clamped so it never strobes. `PLANET_SIZE` scales them up in the default mode.
- **Bulb**: modelled procedurally in Blender by `blender/bulb.py`, an A60 envelope on an E27 cap in real millimetres. Lathe and loft helpers build the envelope, the hollow flare, the press (a tube that morphs into a flat superellipse slab), the arbor and its button; the filament is a helix swept along a sagging crown, held by four molybdenum supports with hooks and two nickel leads with clamps, dumet below the press. The brass cap has a real Edison thread (pitch 3.629 mm) from a radial displacement, three crimp dimples and a rolled lip. `blender/bake.py` bakes colour, roughness, metallic, normal and AO for the cap (lathe marks, pits, tarnish in the thread valleys) and roughness and AO for the black glass insulator. Exported as GLB with meshopt and WebP.
- **Rails**: every marble runs on a bent steel wire track like a hand-soldered marble run: two rods that follow its orbit and cradle it from below, 45 degrees either side, joined every few marble diameters by a little U-shaped tie that dips under the marble, with a blob of solder at each joint (instanced, squashed and stretched along the rod, jittered per joint). A third guard rod on the outside was tried and dropped, two read cleaner. The top four rings (`FINE_RAILS`) run on half-thickness polished silver wire. The marble's place on the orbit comes from Kepler and nothing else; the ring is then turned so that exactly that point is its lowest. A convex plane curve is lowest at the point whose outward normal points downhill, so the rotation is built from two frames: the orbit's plane normal and the outward normal at the marble go to a plane tilted by alpha and that plane's downhill direction (R = B2 B1^T). The orbit's small inclination is absorbed. Alpha follows the planet's angular speed at that point from Kepler's second law, h / r^2 with h = k sqrt(a (1 - e^2)), against Mercury at perihelion, to the power 1/3 (`TILT_EXP`) so the slow outer rings still tilt, capped at 4 degrees: steeper at perihelion and for inner planets, a function of position only, the same paused or running. Checked numerically: every ring's lowest point lands on its marble to within one path sample. The chains read their heights off this rotation. The marble rolls about (plane normal x motion) by distance over radius instead of spinning on an axis. Saturn's ring moved inside its marble, an outer one would hit the rail and roll along with it.
- **Decks**: the rings cascade down like a mobile, the further out the lower. Each orbit steps down from the previous one by a minimum step, or further where its track would overlap an inner one radially, enough to clear it counting marbles, rods and both rings tilting towards each other; `DECK_SPREAD` scales every step. The stack is centred on the bulb, which hangs upside down from a cord.
- **Chains**: the chains are the drive. Each ring hangs by its own four tiny steel chains (`CHAINS_PER_RING`), straight up from little soldered L brackets on the outer rod (out past the marble, then up, so the chain clears it) to winches in the ceiling. Two opposite pairs: reeling them in and out tilts the ring, the tilt rolls the marble, and every ring rolls at its own speed, so no two rings can share chains. As the low point walks round, each chain rides up and down in its own phase. Links are laid from the bracket upwards at a fixed pitch, so a chain moves with its ring like it is being reeled rather than stretching. The cascade keeps them clear: outer rings are lower and wider, so every chain passes outside the rings above it. They differ per chain, rolled from its index: most are fine link chain, some ball chain like a lamp pull, a few heavier link chain, each a little different in gauge. Links are instances of one stretched torus, every other one turned 90 degrees; beads are instanced spheres with a gap.
- **Light**: the bulb is the only light. No ambient, no environment map: a point light with shadows (the bulb's own meshes cast none, the light sits inside the glass) and the filament's bloom. Rails, ties, solder, chains and vanes cast; metal and glass only show the lamp as a highlight, like in a dark room. Decay 1 rather than the physical 2, which would leave the outer rings black. The floor is a plain lit plane with the grid as a canvas texture, so it only shows where the bulb reaches and catches every shadow.
- **Labels**: plain divs moved by transform each frame. drei's `<Html>` makes one React root per label and React 19 complains about it.

## Learned

- `@vitejs/plugin-react` 6 needs Vite 8.
- Solidify with even offset can throw an open rim far away along a bad normal. Turn it off for open shells.
- A plane under drei's `<Grid>` z-fights it at distance. The background is the floor.
- Bloom and tone mapping in the composer also tone map the clear colour: a white page goes grey under any filmic curve. Another reason to go dark.
