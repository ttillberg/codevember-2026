# 08 / Tesseract

2026-11-08

TESSERAKT, a hypercube built for real. A tesseract cannot exist in three dimensions, but its shadow can: turned in 4D and seen in perspective, it is a 3D object whose edges change length and nothing else. So that is the machine. 32 telescopic struts in three stages, sleeve, stanchion, rod, on 16 ball joints, with one crank turning it a quarter at a time through the XW, YW or ZW plane, evenly, or, on M, a servo doing it in steps the way a first prototype moves: hold, snap, overshoot, settle. Radii are constant like on a real part, only the lengths move. Painted sand and graphite with decals, silver rods, black seals, a coloured ring here and there, six blinking LEDs, a quad drone living in the cells with its own camera in the corner, on graph paper. Space stops the crank, the arrows turn it by hand, drag orbits and sets which way it turns, a held pointer is a laser, and it turns round on its own every 8 to 22 s, M swaps the even spin for a stepping servo. `?clean` hides the page furniture.

## How

- **One plane at a time** (`state.ts`): the machine turns in XW, YW or ZW, never two at once. Compose turns in two planes and an edge can point straight at the eye; its strut would have to shrink to nothing. But after a quarter turn the tesseract stands on itself again, a cell has moved to the next place, so the next quarter can go in any plane. The pose is a base of whole quarter turns, a signed permutation of the axes, and one live angle in one plane on top. In one plane the shortest strut is 0.73 and the longest 3.03 (`node` sweep over the whole turn), and no two joints that are not neighbours come closer than 1.0. Seeing it from other sides is a 3D job: orbit.
- **Servo** (`state.ts`): a stiff spring on the crank angle (ω ≈ 19, ζ 0.68), so a step lands with one small overshoot and no wobble. The program steps a quarter turn at a time, the tesseract's own period: every hold is the same shape with the colours moved on. Half the steps come whole, the rest stagger in two or three uneven lurches with a tenth of a second between, then a hold of 0.9 to 2.7 s. One in ten goes back. The arrows move the target, the spring does the rest.
- **Struts** (`Machine.tsx`): per edge, a sleeve sunk into each ball, a stanchion sliding out of each sleeve as far as the strut needs up to 0.8, and a rod between the stanchions taking the rest. Each stage overlaps the one round it by 0.1 so nothing shows daylight. Two sleeves fully compressed are 0.65 with the balls, under the shortest strut. Seals are short fat rings at every mouth.
- **Colour**: the eight edges along w are sand, the other 24 graphite, so you can follow which edges are which as the inner cube turns out and the outer turns in.
- **Decals** (`decals.ts`): canvas textures on the cylinder UVs, not `DecalGeometry`. One sleeve in three has one, the rest are plain paint; x runs round the tube the way a viewer outside reads it, y along it, the open end at the top; the far sleeve of a strut hangs the other way up, so its lettering is turned round. Each carries a few of: hazard band at the mouth, brand round the tube (CDVMBR 08, three.js with its triangle mark, pmndrs), serial along it, barcode, a coloured tab, the three.js mark as pictogram. Seeded, so the same every load.
- **Assembly marks** (`Machine.tsx`): a parting line round every ball with two screws on it, a groove round every sleeve by the ball with a screw beside it. A screw is a steel disc flush with the surface and a darker hex socket in it; the eye reads the recess. All instanced, four draw calls for 272 marks.
- **Drone** (`Drones.tsx`): a small quad, sand body, four dark rotors spinning, a lamp under the belly, lives in the tesseract on its boundary. It is inside one of the eight cells, a cube with one coordinate held at ±1 and three free, and can only leave through a square face into the neighbouring cell: cruise a while, then out through a face in one smooth curve, a two-piece cubic Bezier in 4D with the face at its middle. It flies square into the face and square on out of it, so the turn is the 4D right angle itself, taken in the face; every control point stays on the boundary inside its cell, so the path never bulges out of the tesseract. A Gaussian field round the face takes the speed down to half and gives it back, the lamp breathing brighter through it. At the middle the held coordinate swaps with the face's, and the face itself, the square between its four joints, flashes green just before, in both views. Never straight back out the way it came. Position and trail are points in 4D seen through the same turn and perspective as the struts: what reads as speed is partly the warp of the cell it is in, and the trail bends as the machine turns. In 3D it noses into its projected velocity and leans into the move.
- **Drone cam** (`DroneCam.tsx`, `drone.ts`): a second scene in a scissored viewport, bottom right, an unlabelled frame, from the drone's own camera. It renders cell space: the cube the drone is in, rigid struts, no telescopes, and the six neighbouring cubes continuing straight on through its faces as in a net. From inside, a tesseract is just rooms. A click on the frame swaps the drone's own eyes for a chase camera a little behind and above it, eased, with the quad in the picture; `?follow` starts there. Cell space is an affine map from 4D, one row per free axis; on crossing a face the row that mapped the face's axis is remade to map the axis that was held, pointing inward and shifted two cells, so the view carries on without a jump. Edges along w are sand here too. The main scene's environment is shared. Hidden in `?clean`.
- **Laser** (`Laser.tsx`): hold the pointer still for a beat and a pink beam from a hand-held pointer, low and to the right of the camera, aims at the point under the pointer, on the first of the 24 projected faces its ray meets, and goes straight on through the machine. At every wall it crosses it is displaced: a short jog sideways along that wall's 4D normal as it projects right now, the fixed axis that lies most across the beam, pointing inward, and then on parallel to itself at half the strength, fading towards the paper. Straight, stepped and dimmed at every wall, like light through a stack of glass; the steps turn with the machine because the normals do. No post bloom on a transparent canvas: the glow is the beam drawn three times, a hot core and two soft wide halos, and the dots the same. A first version took the 4D right angle at each wall like the drone does, which read as random kinks, and a second kept the beam parallel by pulling its direction back through each cell's Jacobian, which falls apart because the cells overlap in projection.
- **LEDs** (`Machine.tsx`): six status lights on the balls, each with its own blink: heartbeat, breathe, a flash every five seconds, fast steady, slow square, tick. A pinhead emissive bead in a black collar, a few pixels, no glow.
- **Light**: drei `Environment` from four `Lightformer`s, no HDR download, plus one directional. `ContactShadows` on a transparent canvas, the graph paper is CSS. Tone mapping is `NeutralToneMapping` at exposure 1: ACES darkened the sand and greyed the accents, Neutral keeps the paint near its hex values, which is the point of a flat-paint look.

## Learned

- Perspective along w is what gives the w-edges length at all; in parallel projection they vanish. The eye distance is a trade between long w-edges (near) and a tame length range (far).
- `setFromUnitVectors(Y, dir)` and the same quaternion times `(1,0,0,0)` gives the strut's two ends the same roll, so decals on both sleeves line up. The far end is upside down though, and upside-down lettering looks mirrored at a glance: check the UVs before flipping the canvas.
