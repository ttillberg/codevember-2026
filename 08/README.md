# 08 / Tesseract

2026-11-08

TESSERAKT, a hypercube built for real. A tesseract cannot exist in three dimensions, but its shadow can: turned in 4D and seen in perspective, it is a 3D object whose edges change length and nothing else. So that is the machine. 32 telescopic struts in three stages, sleeve, stanchion, rod, on 16 ball joints, with one crank turning it a quarter at a time through the XW, YW or ZW plane: a glide of a quarter at each catch, or in hard mode an even turn all the time, broken now and then by a servo doing it in quick steps the way a first prototype moves: snap, overshoot, settle. Radii are constant like on a real part, only the lengths move. Painted sand and graphite with decals, silver rods, black seals, a coloured ring here and there, six blinking LEDs, a quad drone living in the cells with its own camera in the corner, on a soft gradient. Drag orbits the camera round it; the crank turns round on its own every 8 to 22 s. `?clean` hides the page furniture.

## How

- **One plane at a time** (`state.ts`): the machine turns in XW, YW or ZW, never two at once. Compose turns in two planes and an edge can point straight at the eye; its strut would have to shrink to nothing. But after a quarter turn the tesseract stands on itself again, a cell has moved to the next place, so the next quarter can go in any plane. The pose is a base of whole quarter turns, a signed permutation of the axes, and one live angle in one plane on top. In one plane the shortest strut is 0.73 and the longest 3.03 (`node` sweep over the whole turn), and no two joints that are not neighbours come closer than 1.0. Seeing it from other sides is a 3D job: orbit.
- **Servo** (`state.ts`): a spring on the crank angle (ω ≈ 12.6, ζ 0.68), so a step lands with one small overshoot and no wobble. It only works in hard mode: every 6 to 14 s the even spin stops dead and the servo takes over for a burst, a snap to the next quarter the way it was turning, then one to three more quarter turns, the tesseract's own period, half of them whole and the rest staggered in two or three uneven lurches a breath apart. Landed and settled, the quarters are banked, the plane changes and the spin picks up again from still.
- **Struts** (`Machine.tsx`): per edge, a sleeve sunk into each ball, a stanchion sliding out of each sleeve as far as the strut needs up to 0.8, and a rod between the stanchions taking the rest. Each stage overlaps the one round it by 0.1 so nothing shows daylight. Two sleeves fully compressed are 0.65 with the balls, under the shortest strut. Seals are short fat rings at every mouth.
- **Colour**: the eight edges along w are sand (white was tried for a while), the other 24 graphite, so you can follow which edges are which as the inner cube turns out and the outer turns in.
- **Decals** (`decals.ts`): canvas textures on the cylinder UVs, not `DecalGeometry`. One sleeve in three has one, the rest are plain paint; x runs round the tube the way a viewer outside reads it, y along it, the open end at the top; the far sleeve of a strut hangs the other way up, so its lettering is turned round. Each carries a few of: hazard band at the mouth, brand round the tube (CDVMBR 08, three.js with its triangle mark, pmndrs), serial along it, barcode, a coloured tab, the three.js mark as pictogram. Seeded, so the same every load.
- **Assembly marks** (`Machine.tsx`): a parting line round every ball with two screws on it, a groove round every sleeve by the ball with a screw beside it. A screw is a steel disc flush with the surface and a darker hex socket in it; the eye reads the recess. All instanced, four draw calls for 272 marks.
- **Device** (`Drones.tsx`, `device.ts`, modelled in Blender, `model/device.blend`, exported to `public/device.glb`): a ball with one principal eye, a graphite core, a blue collar, two green ring pods on its sides, and round it four shell petals hinged at the back: its walls, its wings. In flight they are half shut, spread wide only for the catch, from closing in on the piece till it is taken; going through a face they swing forward and shut the ball with only the eye out, and open again on the far side. Eight blades round the eye are its shutter, a blink at every crossing and now and then on its own. The hinges are nodes in the file, each turned by one angle from code. It lives in the tesseract on its boundary: inside one of the eight cells, a cube with one coordinate held at ±1 and three free, and can only leave through a square face into the neighbouring cell. It goes for a piece: a small hexagonal bit of steel, set in a cell two walls away, never the one it is in; the plan to it is two faces, each placed a little towards the piece on the axes the cells share, then the last stretch, each step made into a leg from wherever the device is when its turn comes, so the flight is one continuous curve. The catch runs in phases: the device holds still in front of the piece for a moment, eye on it, the scoop out; the grip is a scoop as for ice cream, a steel bowl on an arm out of the eye cap with a blade nested in it, which swings over the bowl's mouth on two pins and shuts the piece in, and the scoop goes home shut, the piece swallowed, while the shutter blinks; then the machine glides one quarter turn, eased in and out over two seconds, its only movement, while the next piece emerges two walls off; a half second still, and off again. Between catches the tesseract holds its shape. The next one emerges two walls off, and in the cam the piece sits in its own room of the net, by its cell's map. Through a face a leg is one smooth curve, a two-piece cubic Bezier in 4D with the face at its middle. It flies square into the face and square on out of it, so the turn is the 4D right angle itself, taken in the face; every control point stays on the boundary inside its cell, so the path never bulges out of the tesseract. It flies at a constant speed, by distance along the path (each leg keeps a table of length against its Bezier parameter, which runs fast on the straight and slow in the bends), through walls and all; only the last stretch to the piece slows, over its last 0.4, to a quarter, for the catch. A dip of a third at the face, then a rush through it at two and five times, were tried. The legs are rounder sweeps: the heading carries 0.45 of a leg into it, and a cruise has no random knot in its middle. A Gaussian field round the face still shuts the wings and the ball. At the middle the held coordinate swaps with the face's, and the face itself, the square between its four joints, carries a honeycomb of hairlines in the colour of the cell beyond as the device heads for it (cells 0.012 across a wall 2 across, ten times finer than at first, some 33,000 edges worked out once on the wall and cut to its square; too many to put through the turn on the CPU, so each end keeps its place on the wall and the vertex shader puts it through the turn and the w perspective, from the wall's middle and its two ways along it, turned, as uniforms; grid and ripple share the one geometry, and the frame time does not move. Up close a fine mesh, from afar it reads as a tinted pane), and after the crossing the ripple runs through that honeycomb, each edge lighting as the wave from the crossing reaches it, a faint wash behind (two hairline rings before, a filled ring in a shader before them); in the eye's view the old filled flash, for now. Never straight back out the way it came. Position and trail are points in 4D seen through the same turn and perspective as the struts: what reads as speed is partly the warp of the cell it is in, and the trail bends as the machine turns. Its orientation lives in cell space, the way it is going with an up carried from frame to frame, or the pilot's; a cell's map takes the eye's forward and up to 4D and the projection at the device gives the 3D pose. Through a wall it is two copies: in 4D the half of the body past the wall lies in the neighbour's space and projects with the neighbour's turn, so a second copy is posed by the neighbour's map and the two are clipped against the wall's plane, this side and the far side, and fold at it. Before it, a quad drone with rotors. Its size in the picture, and the piece's with it, is two thirds of what it was at first, made a third and then twice that (SIZE_MAIN, PIECE_R): the reach, the catch and the hand-over at a wall go by its radius and come down with it; the eye's view keeps its own size.
- **Two modes** (`p`, or the CONTROLS switch under MODE bottom left, the same size as the small ones (it was one and a half times them), every switch its name and then ON | OFF at the end of the line, outlined, the one it is at filled grey (a knob in a track before, white when on for a while, then no fill), ON is manual, OFF is auto; at the controls a LOCK POINTER button over the keys on the left (taking the controls locking it straight away was tried and dropped), small, lime with an ink border and a crosshair, does what `l` does, and while locked it gives way to a hint, ESC to unlock, there being no pointer to click with; at the controls the keys sit bottom left), the same two views in both: the machine fills the page, the eye's view fills the whole page behind it, fading in from nothing at the left edge and at most 0.4 strong at the right, a ground (it was the right third, full strength), a drag on the page orbits the camera and coasts. Auto, the attract mode: the device flies on its own and the camera turns slowly on a turntable, its look leaning a third of the way from the machine's middle to the device, eased, so it follows the beetle without chasing it. MODE and SETTINGS bottom left, as wide as the blurb (330 px), the shortcuts bottom right, flush with the edge and mirrored, what each does on the left and the keys on the right, with no heading and no group names, only the gaps between the groups kept, the alt-drag simply TRANSFORM (it was Twist the tesseract, by an edge if over one), straight under the orbit (or the rotate), not a group of its own (SHORTCUTS, marked (interactive mode) at the controls, and NAVIGATION, VIEW and OPTIONS over the groups, were dropped as plain enough) (one panel bottom left at first, then spread over the right and three corners for a while), each with a hairline under its name: under MODE the CONTROLS switch and at the controls the lock pointer button; under SETTINGS two small switches, both on to start: TURNTABLE, and FOLLOW, off to start and on its own only (at the controls the switch goes and the camera is the usual one), a smooth follow and nothing more: the same orbit, round the beetle instead of the machine's middle, at half the distance, the look easing after it, the way round the orbit's own yaw and pitch (the turntable and a drag turn it, the machine's turn in 4D does not), never more than about 50 degrees up or down. Tried before it and dropped, all too much: hard cuts on the machine's beat and at every wall; then shots in turn, from the back, at 45 degrees, from the side and from above, glided between, with a close-up on the catch and a shoulder sway. TRANSFORM, off to start (it was on), the turntable with it, one switch for both (TURNTABLE had its own for a while, on to start), hard mode made soft: the machine turns in 4D all the time, evenly at 0.22 rad/s, changing plane at each quarter, with no servo cutting in, and the catches no longer glide a quarter since it turns anyway. CAMERA GRID, on to start (for a while off to start and set by the CONTROLS switch): off, the eye's view is skipped whole, just the page and the machine (hiding only the rooms left the piece and its bits floating there). With alt held, and only then, a strut under the pointer gets an outline, pale lime, an inverted hull round it (`Grip.tsx`); found with no raycast, the 16 joints projected to the page and the nearest strut's line within a few pixels, the ends by the balls left to the balls. Drag it and the machine turns by it, the lime full: the plane is picked by the drag, as alt does, so along the strut it slides on its own axis and across it on one of the other two, up and down another turn than left and right; the sign is set so the strut goes with the pointer, an outer cube's strut the other way to an inner one's. Manual: the device keeps the way it was going and from then on has momentum in cell space: a drag anywhere on the page is the look, as in a first-person game, or with the pointer locked (`l`) the mouse itself is; with shift held it orbits the machine instead, and in either mode with alt held it turns the machine in 4D by hand (the hand in `App.tsx`, `crankStart` `crankBy` `crankEnd` in `state.ts`), taking over from a glide, a burst or the spin. The drag picks the plane by the way it goes: the machine's three axes are seen on the page and the one the drag runs along most, weighted by how long it looks, turns with w, so the cells flow the way it is dragged; from then on only the part of the drag along that axis counts. It can only pick while the machine stands on a whole quarter, since another plane part way through a turn is another pose; let go and the servo takes it on to the nearest quarter with its small overshoot, so the next drag is free again; `w` `s` and the up and down arrows go forward and back along the eye, `a` `d` and the side arrows strafe, `space` is forward too, all against drag. Hold the piece in the lock (its cell, close, ahead of the eye) for half a second and the device docks on its own: it turns its eye on the piece, closes to a scoop's reach, holds still a beat, scoops it. Hard mode (`h`, or the HARD MODE switch under CONTROLS, hidden for now), in either: the machine turns in 4D all the time, changing plane at each quarter, and every so often the servo cuts in with a burst of quick quarter turns. Through a wall the body is two copies, one in each cell's pose, each clipped to its own side of the wall: by default both at once, the halves of one body folded at the wall; with `o`, kept off the key bar and off the settings, in-then-out, where the body sinks all the way into the wall before the next cell's copy rises out of it, set back and catching up; the copy going in fades as it sinks (made transparent only while it fades, each material from its own opacity); the gap and the rise are distances, counted from the body all through the wall, 1.5 and 4 radii (seconds were tried and lost the in, then out); (a switch for it was tried and dropped). The eye cam looks where the eye points. Through a wall it is the same crossing as on its own, the room beyond is the next cell, and the wall ahead lights as it closes in; the field round the walls slows it and shuts the ball as it does on its own. `p` again lets go and it carries on from there by itself. The crossing rule in `drone.ts` moves the net by the wall's side in cell space, not its 4D sign: a row can run its axis backwards after a crossing, and the old rule put the next room on the wrong side then.
- **Plate** (`App.tsx`, `style.css`): 08 TESSERACT in IBM Plex Sans bold, CODEVEMBER 2026 under it a quarter the size, and under that a blurb in small grey Helvetica, two short paragraphs: what a tesseract is and that we see its shadow, a cube in a cube; and what the turn and the beetle show (rigid in 4D yet the inner cube swells out; a physical object to illustrate it, the beetle, its position and rotation transposed from 4D, searching for blinking steel balls to catch, the line drawing behind its view, each cell a true cube from inside, to navigate by with the controls on). Five longer ones, with the counts, the paper analogy and how to play, were cut down; on a phone none.
- **Depth of field** (`dof.ts`): by hand, not a post-processing pass, which would draw the frame opaque over the eye's lens and the page. The machine renders alone into a half-float buffer with its depth and alpha, then onto the page through a gather blur, a golden-angle spiral of 48 taps, each counted as far as its own blur reaches back, held behind sharper points so the background does not bleed over the focus; tone mapping and the output colours applied in that last pass. Focus on what the camera looks at, the beetle when following, a stronger blur there, as a lens focused near.
- **Eye cam** (`DroneCam.tsx`, `drone.ts`): a second scene in a scissored viewport, the whole page behind the machine, fading in from the left edge and never more than 0.4 strong, always there, `?clean` too, from the device's eye. It renders cell space: the cube the device is in, no telescopes, drawn as outlines now, the inverted hull pushed out by an angle (its distance from the eye times 0.0022) so the ink is a hairline near and far, through a 1024 cube map (it was drawn three ways, `g` round them, and the key went; the hull was a fixed 0.013 thick, fat up close): of the three, the drawing, as an architect's: ink-outlined tubes, a paper fill and an inverted hull round it in the cube map, with over them each edge's centre line, a dash-dot hairline in a lighter grey running on through the joints, and a detail circle round every joint, the piece a ball outlined like the tubes; or the outlined tubes alone; or the edges as true hairlines, a pixel wide. Through the cube map a pixel line faded to nothing, blown up by the lens, so the edges skip it: each is cut in 24 pieces and put straight through the same equidistant lens in a vertex shader, at the screen's own resolution, after the lens has drawn the rest (the piece, the wall's ripple); fog and the panel's fade in the shader. Each joint has a hairline circle round it, built in the same shader facing the eye, and the piece is a geodesic ball in hairlines, all one graphite, its steel and lamps left out here. The outlines came first, the hairlines after, and the outlines won, and a box of 5 × 5 × 5 rooms round it continuing straight on through the faces as in a net, instanced, so when the net recentres on a new room the only cubes born are a whole slab on the far side, four units out at the least, already in the fog. From inside, a tesseract is just rooms. The eye is a 180 degree fisheye: the room is rendered into a cube map from the eye's position each frame and unwrapped onto the frame by an equidistant lens, a right angle out to each side edge, the disc made big enough to cover the whole panel, so only its middle shows; fog and tone mapping as in the main picture. The view keeps its own up vector from frame to frame and only leans back to the room's up while looking level, so a leg straight up or down a room never flips it; the orientation is slerped so no frame can snap. Cell space is an affine map from 4D, one row per free axis; on crossing a face the row that mapped the face's axis is remade to map the axis that was held, pointing inward and shifted two cells, so the view carries on without a jump. All one graphite here, a fine line, no colour along w, and exponential fog the colour of the backdrop lets the rooms beyond fade. The main scene's environment is shared. Hidden in `?clean`.
- **Wake** (`smoke.ts`, `Drones.tsx`): clouds of point-sprite puffs left in 4D behind the beetle, tinted with the cell they were left in: a cluster every 2.5 to 6 hundredths (it was 5 to 12), three to six small puffs in a large faint one, and a steady line of small ones between; up to 2,400 alive, eight seconds each, at 0.16 strength. The thin laser line through it (`laser.ts`) is hidden, behind a flag, the clouds carry the way it came.
- **Lasers**, tried and dropped: beams fixed in the room through the machine, cast against the 24 projected faces and jogged along each wall's projected 4D normal, three layers for a fake bloom, shown in the drone cam too. Handsome in stills, but it fought the machine for the eye. Lives in the history before `08/src/Laser.tsx` was removed.
- **LEDs** (`Machine.tsx`): six status lights on the balls, each with its own blink: heartbeat, breathe, a flash every five seconds, fast steady, slow square, tick. A pinhead emissive bead in a black collar, a few pixels, no glow.
- **Light**: drei `Environment` from four `Lightformer`s, no HDR download, plus one directional. `ContactShadows` on a transparent canvas; behind it a soft radial gradient in CSS, light at the machine, darker at the edges. Tone mapping is `NeutralToneMapping` at exposure 1: ACES darkened the sand and greyed the accents, Neutral keeps the paint near its hex values, which is the point of a flat-paint look.

## Learned

- Perspective along w is what gives the w-edges length at all; in parallel projection they vanish. The eye distance is a trade between long w-edges (near) and a tame length range (far).
- `setFromUnitVectors(Y, dir)` and the same quaternion times `(1,0,0,0)` gives the strut's two ends the same roll, so decals on both sleeves line up. The far end is upside down though, and upside-down lettering looks mirrored at a glance: check the UVs before flipping the canvas.
